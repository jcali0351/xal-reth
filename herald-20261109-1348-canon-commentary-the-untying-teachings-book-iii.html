Canon Commentary: The Untying (Teachings, Book III, Chapter 2)

Nhalor has never cut anything. A slow reading of the Veiled Scroll's second chapter, on untying a rope instead of cutting it, with notes for anyone retiring a main this Shedfall.

The second chapter of the Veiled Scroll is seven verses long and is read at dusk, quietly, with the palms facing down. It is also, by the Ledger's count, the chapter most often requested by faithful who have just left a raid team. We offer this commentary for them, and for anyone else holding a rope they are about to set down.

Nhalor does not end things. He lets things end.

The Warm-Blooded imagine a god of endings carrying a blade. The Teachings refuse the blade entirely. There is, the chapter says, a difference between cutting a rope and untying it, and Nhalor has never cut anything. Not once. The Listeners have checked.

On the frayed end

To cut is quick. It leaves a frayed end on both sides, and frayed ends catch on things for years. Every faithful has met a cut rope: the guildmate who left mid-dungeon without a word, the friendship that ended in a single whisper at three in the morning, the main deleted in a mood and mourned by Tuesday. Grippers love a frayed end. It is something to hold.

To untie is slow. It leaves the rope whole, so it can be used again. This is the part the commentators linger on: an untied friendship can be tied again later, looser, at a different post. A cut one needs a knot in the middle forever.

On naming what it gave

The chapter's only instruction is small. Before you set a thing down, name what it gave you, out loud. The Inner Scale suggests three things, no more, so the rite does not become a speech. A faithful retiring a main this season was heard on the Warm Ridge saying: "You taught me to interrupt. You carried me through two expansions. You looked good in that hat." Then he logged out, and the Wardens report he looked lighter.

Scalekeeper Orrin the Slow, who keeps the cord in the Hall of Waiting, ties one loose knot for each thing named and lets the newest egg untie them all. Nobody remembers why. It is now tradition, which is how most traditions begin.

Practice for the week

  • Find one thing you are holding by its frayed end.
  • Name what it gave you. Three things.
  • Untie it slowly. Leave the rope whole.

"I do not take," Nhalor says. "I only hold the door while you put it down." Read the rest of the Veiled Scroll in the Teachings of the Sacred Triangle.

Hold light. And let go well. Glory to Xal'Reth.
