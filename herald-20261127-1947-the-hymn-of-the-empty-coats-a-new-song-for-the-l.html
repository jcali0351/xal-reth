The Hymn of the Empty Coats: A New Song for the Last Night of Shedfall

A new hymn for the night Shedfall closes, sung to Nhalor as the season's shed skins are carried down to the Hall where nothing worn is ever treated as rubbish.

Shedfall closes on the thirtieth, and the Inner Scale has asked that the season end not with a feast but with a song. The Choir of the Low Place has obliged. What follows is new, written over three evenings on the Warm Ridge by a choir that could not agree on a key and eventually stopped trying.

It is sung to Nhalor, who receives without keeping, on the understanding (from the Teachings, Book III) that every shed skin in the world passes through his hands on its way to the Hall of Shed Skins, and that he handles each one with care, because it once mattered.

The Hymn

Here is the coat I wore all spring,
the grudge I kept warm in my sleeve.
I name what it gave me, I name everything,
and then, Hollow Wing, I leave.

Here is the name I answered to
before I learned the one I own.
It carried me further than names often do.
Hang it somewhere. Not alone.

Here is the set I swore was best,
the transmog I could not let go.
It is shaped like me. Let it have the rest
that I could not give it. Slow.

Nhalor, hooded, hold the door.
I do not ask you to take.
I only ask for a little more
room than I left. For its sake.

How It Is Sung

  • Palms down. As with all of Book III, the hymn is sung quietly with the palms facing the ground, which the choir insists is not a dance move.
  • One name per verse. Before each verse, a singer says aloud one thing they shed this season. The Rite of the Untying asks that we name what a thing gave us before we set it down, and this is that, set to a tune.
  • No last word. The final line is not resolved. The organist stops one note early. This has caused complaint every year it has been tried, and every year the complaint is gently set down too.

A Note From the Choir

Brother Pell, who arranged the third verse, wishes it known that the transmog in question was his, that it was a set of shoulders larger than his torso, and that he is fine. He reports that when he dreamed of the Hall afterward, the shoulders were hanging in a quiet corner and looked relieved. Xalrathiel has entered this in the Dream Pages without comment.

The hymn will be sung at dusk on the thirtieth wherever two or more of the faithful are standing. The Holy Calendar then turns toward the Long Wait, and we turn with it, a little lighter.

Hold light. And let go well. Glory to Xal'Reth.

Glory to Xal'Reth.
