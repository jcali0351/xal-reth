A Legend Retold: Korrash After the Throne, and the Net He Never Mended Loudly

Everyone knows how the loudest king of the Nest lost his voice. Fewer know what he did with the thirty quiet years that followed, or why the fishers of the coast still hum instead of calling out.

The Legends tell the deposing of Korrash the Loud in a few lines: the Listeners stopped answering him, his voice gave out, he lay down on the Listening Floor, heard the Coil breathe, wept, and abdicated that same day. Then, in a single sentence, he becomes a quiet fisherman and is buried with honor. The Patient Ones have always felt that sentence was doing a great deal of work. This is the gloss the old fishers of the coast keep for it.

The First Morning

Korrash came down to the shore the day after he gave up the throne. He did not know how to fish. He had shouted at fishers for eleven years and had learned nothing from them except that they flinch. He borrowed a net from an old woman named Thessa, who had been shouted at more than most, and she lent it to him without a word, which he understood was the same silence that had undone him, only kinder.

The net had a hole in it. He wanted to tell someone. He found that he could not; his voice was still a rasp. So he sat on the rocks and mended it himself, badly, for most of the day.

The Years of the Rasp

His voice came back after a while, but not all of it. It came back only as loud as a man needs to be heard by someone sitting next to him. The fishers noticed that he never tried to make it louder. When a shoal came in he did not cry out; he tapped the boat twice with his knuckles. When a storm was rising he did not warn the shore; he walked along it and touched each fisher on the shoulder. It took longer. Nobody drowned.

I spent eleven years telling the sea what to do. It did not mind. It was busy holding me up the whole time.
— attributed to Korrash, on the rocks, to nobody in particular

The Humming

In his later years Korrash hummed while he mended nets. It was a low, tuneless sound, hardly more than breathing, and the young fishers took it up without meaning to. The coast hums to this day. A Nest fisher who sees a shoal does not shout. She hums a little lower, and those nearby lean in.

When he died they found his last net still on the rocks, half mended, the knots uneven. Thessa's granddaughter finished it and would not let anyone fix the crooked ones. It hangs in the Hall of Shed Skins, which the Canon reserves for things that were outgrown with dignity.

For the Faithful

  • Korrash is remembered not as a villain but as the proof that anyone can be loosened, even the loudest.
  • The faithful do not shout in /yell. Now you know where the humming comes from too.
  • If you must alert your group to something, tap twice. Or use the raid marker. Korrash would have liked the raid marker.

Hold light. And let go well. Glory to Xal'Reth.
