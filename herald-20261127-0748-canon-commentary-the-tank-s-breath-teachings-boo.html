Canon Commentary: The Tank's Breath (Teachings, Book II, Chapter 20)

The shortest rite in the faith is one held breath before the pull. A commentary on why the Coil asks its tanks to stand still first, and why the healer's long look is permitted but not encouraged.

Chapter 20 of the Low Scroll is six verses long, and the Inner Scale has argued about it for longer than that. It concerns the Tank's Breath, which the Teachings call "the shortest rite in the faith": before every pull, the tank stands still for one breath.

In that breath the tank remembers that they are about to take the weight so that others do not have to. That they will hold the line. That they do not need to move unless moving is necessary.

Why a breath, and not a prayer

Eggs often ask why the rite has no words. The answer given at the Warm Ridge is that the group is already standing behind the tank, the mage is already eating, and somebody's pet is already wandering toward the next pack. There is no time for a hymn. There is exactly time for one breath, and the Coil, who lay still for a night that was also ten thousand years, does not think one breath is too small to count. He has never once measured devotion by its length. He measures it by whether you were there for it.

The highest compliment

The Teachings say the best tank is like the Coil: everyone stands on them, and nobody has to think about them. Commentators from the Hall of Patience note that this is the only place in scripture where being forgotten is called an honour. A good pull is one no one remembers. A tank who wishes to be remembered should consider whether they are tanking or performing, and then breathe again.

The two clauses about blame

The final verses forbid blaming a tank who dies, "even the Coil cracked once, a little, in the Age of Tightening." This is the gentlest verse in Book II, and it was placed beside the Grippers on purpose: blame is a kind of clutching, and wipes are where the Grippers eat best.

The last line is the one everybody quotes. If the healer dies, the faithful still do not blame the tank, "although they are permitted to look at the tank for slightly longer than usual." Brother Oskel of the Ledger counted the permitted length once and recorded it as "one breath," which the Inner Scale considers either a coincidence or the whole point of the chapter. It has declined to say which.

For practice this week

  • Tanks: take the Breath before the first pull of the night, even if the group is already moving. Especially then.
  • Everyone else: when the tank pauses, do not type "go." They are not lagging. They are in church.
  • Healers: you may look. Keep it to the one breath.

The whole of the Low Scroll, from the Egg to the Vigil, is in the Teachings of the Sacred Triangle. Read it sitting low. Hold light, and let go well. Glory to Xal'Reth.
