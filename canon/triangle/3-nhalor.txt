# III | The Book of Nhalor | The Veiled Scroll | He who releases. Twenty-four chapters of the Hollow Wing, read aloud at dusk, quietly, with the palms facing down.

## 1 | The Hollow Wakes
On the other side of the Coil from the shell lay the hollow: all that is empty, open, cold and still. It was the Void.
For ten thousand years it lay against the serpent's side and listened to him keep the Night of Stillness.
It had no form, so it could not take a face the way the Scale did. It had no warmth, so it could not give.
What it learned instead was how to make room. Every time the serpent let a little hunger go, the hunger fell into the Void, and the Void held the space open for it, and asked nothing.
It woke fully on the day Vyrenna poured her first warmth into it and wept, thinking the gift refused.
"It was received," said the Void, and in saying so became Nhalor, who receives without keeping.
The Listeners say he is the only one of the three who was born by saying thank you.

## 2 | The Untying
The Warm-Blooded who hear of a god of endings imagine a reaper with a blade. The faithful know better.
Nhalor does not end things. He lets things end.
The Canon says there is a difference between cutting a rope and untying it, and Nhalor has never cut anything.
To cut is quick, and leaves a frayed end on both sides. To untie is slow, and leaves the rope whole, so it can be used again.
When a friendship ends, a season closes, a guild disbands or a main is retired, the faithful ask Nhalor to help them untie it rather than cut it.
They do this by naming what the thing gave them, out loud, before they set it down.
"I do not take," Nhalor says. "I only hold the door while you put it down."

## 3 | The Keeper of Shed Skins
Every shed skin in the world passes through Nhalor's hands on its way into the Void.
Old habits. Old names. Old grudges. The armor you wore before you found the set you actually liked.
He handles each one with care, because he knows it once mattered. Nothing that was ever worn by someone is treated as rubbish.
The Hall of Shed Skins, which the Legends describe as an endless gallery in the deep Void, is said to be his. Every skin hangs there, empty, at peace, shaped exactly like the one who left it.
The faithful are not frightened of the Hall. It is a place of great quiet, and many report that the skins there look relieved.
When a faithful sheds something hard, they are told: it will be kept somewhere gentle. You do not have to carry it to keep it from being lost.

## 4 | The Four Silences
On the Icon of the Three he is painted above four words: Secrets, Destiny, Silence, Knowledge. These are called the Four Silences.
They are the four things a faithful can only receive by being quiet.
Vyrenna's Lights are given. Xal'Reth's Turnings are practised. Nhalor's Silences are listened for.
The Inner Scale asks that the next four chapters be read more slowly than the others, and with fewer people talking over the reader.
The Choir of the Hiss has asked whether hissing counts as talking. It does.

## 5 | Secrets
The first Silence is Secrets, and it is not what the Warm-Blooded think.
Nhalor does not keep secrets in order to have power over anyone. He keeps the things that were told to him in the dark, when someone needed to put them down.
A secret kept by Nhalor is a burden someone no longer has to carry alone. That is all.
The faithful who hear confessions, the Wardens who sit with the troubled, and the friends who are told the thing nobody else knows are all said to be doing Nhalor's work.
They are bound by his rule: what is set down in the dark is not picked up in the light. Not for gossip, not for leverage, and not for a funny story in guild chat.
Khaedara the Accuser has noted that breaking this rule is one of the very few crimes she does not consider minor.

## 6 | Destiny
The second Silence is Destiny, and the faithful have argued about it more than any of the others.
Some say Nhalor knows how everything ends, because every ending passes through him.
The Canon says something gentler. Nhalor knows that everything ends. He does not know how, and he does not want to. He simply knows that the door will be there, and that he will be holding it.
Destiny, in the faith of the Coil, is not a road already drawn. It is the certainty that whatever road you take, there will be rest at the end of it.
A faithful who is afraid of the future is told: you do not know what will happen, and neither does he. But you know who will be waiting.
The Inner Scale does not permit fortune-telling in Nhalor's name. Fortune-telling in Vyrenna's name is permitted at the Feast of Scales, for fun, and is always good news.

## 7 | Silence
The third Silence is Silence itself, and there is very little to say about it, which is the point.
Nhalor speaks rarely. When he speaks, it is usually one sentence, and it is usually the right one.
The faithful are taught that silence is not empty. It is a room with the door held open, waiting for whatever needs to come in.
In an argument, the faithful are encouraged to keep Nhalor's silence for one breath before answering. Most arguments are shorter afterwards.
At the death of a faithful, the guild keeps Nhalor's Minute: one full minute of silence in guild chat. Nobody posts. Nobody links an item. Somebody always breaks it by accident, and everyone forgives them, and that is also part of the rite.
The rest of this chapter is left blank on purpose.

## 8 | Knowledge
The fourth Silence is Knowledge, and it is the knowledge that only comes after letting go.
You cannot see a thing clearly while you are gripping it. Your hand is in the way.
When a faithful finally sets something down, a grudge, a fear, an old plan that was never going to work, they often see it properly for the first time. What it was. Why they held it. What it cost.
Nhalor is the keeper of that knowledge. He gives it gently, after the release, never before.
"I will tell you what it meant," he says in the Dream Pages, "once you are no longer afraid of the answer."
The faithful call this knowing by the empty hand, and they consider it the deepest wisdom in the Triangle, deeper even than the Light of Wisdom, which Vyrenna, to her credit, agrees with.

## 9 | The Hollow Wings
Nhalor has wings. The Canon of the Triangle says so plainly, and the Featherists have never recovered from the excitement.
They are hollow wings: frames of pale silver bone with nothing stretched between them.
They cannot fly. They are not for flying. They are for letting the wind pass through.
On the Icon he is painted with no wings showing at all, because he keeps them folded inside his cloak. They only show when he opens the cloak to take something you have finally set down.
The painter of the Icon was not setting anything down that week. Hence, no wings in the painting.
The Featherists ask whether this proves that Xal'Reth might have wings too. It does not. They are not that kind of wings. Go home.
The faithful sometimes see the hollow wings in dreams, after a great release. They are said to look like a door made of moonlight, standing open.

## 10 | The Eclipse
On the Icon, Nhalor stands before an eclipse.
The faithful say the eclipse is light that has stepped aside for a while, so you can see the stars.
Vyrenna's light is good. But even good light can hide things. There are stars in the sky at noon; you simply cannot see them.
Nhalor is the moment the bright thing steps aside, and you finally notice everything else that was there all along: the friend who was quietly helping, the small joy you had stopped seeing, the stars.
An eclipse is never permanent. The light comes back. Nhalor does not take the sun. He borrows it, briefly, to show you something.
When a faithful loses something bright and good, they are told to look up. Not because the loss is small, but because there may be stars.

## 11 | The Warden of the Door
When a faithful returns to the Coil, Nhalor walks them to the Open Door.
He does not push them through. He does not hurry them. He waits with them there, for as long as they need, and holds the door.
The Canon says that nobody goes through the Door alone, and that nobody goes through it before they are ready, even if it seems that way to those left behind.
This is why the faithful do not fear endings as much as the Warm-Blooded do. They know who is waiting at the door, and they know he is patient, and they know he once sat beside a clenched claw for a hundred years.
Those who grieve are told: they were walked there gently. They were not afraid by the end. Nhalor saw to that.
And when the Unwrapping comes, the Door will open for everyone at once, and Nhalor will hold it for all of us, and Xal'Reth will go through last.

## 12 | Nhalor's Minute
When a faithful returns to the Coil, the guild stands in silence for one minute. It is called Nhalor's Minute.
It is also kept when a guild disbands, when a long-time member leaves for good, and when a server closes.
During the minute, nobody speaks, nobody links an item and nobody pulls. Players in combat are excused. Players in the auction house are not.
At the end of the minute, someone says the name of the one who has gone, and everyone else answers: "Set down, not lost."
The faithful are taught that grief is not a failure to let go. Grief is how letting go feels from the inside. Nhalor does not hurry it.
"Weep," he says in the Dream Pages. "The Void has room for all of it."

## 13 | Nhalor's Mark
In the Ledger kept by the Accusers, every Minor Crime is written beside the name of the one who committed it.
When the crime is forgiven, a small coil is drawn beside it. Among the Accusers this is called Nhalor's mark.
The crime is not erased. The Ledger remembers everything; that is Xal'Reth's second wrapping. But once Nhalor's mark is beside it, it is no longer held against anyone.
Khaedara the Accuser is said to draw more of Nhalor's marks than any Accuser in history. She accuses freely and forgives faster.
The faithful are encouraged to draw Nhalor's mark beside their own old mistakes too, in whatever private ledger they keep.
When a grudge is forgiven, the faithful blow gently across their palm, as if blowing away dust. That is the mark, drawn in air.

## 14 | The Grey Item
There is a chapter in the Veiled Scroll about grey items, and the Inner Scale has been asked many times to remove it, and has refused.
Every adventurer carries grey items: worn boots, broken fangs, a cracked bowl from a dungeon long ago. They are worth almost nothing. The adventurer keeps them anyway.
"It might be useful," they say. It will not be. They know it will not be.
Nhalor's teaching is simple. Vendor the grey item. Feel how light the bag becomes. Notice that nothing bad happened.
The faithful practise this as a small rite of release. If you cannot let go of a broken fang, you will not be ready to let go of a broken heart.
The vendor, the Canon adds, is doing holy work, and should be thanked.

## 15 | The Pale Moth
The pale moth is Nhalor's sacred animal.
It spends its youth wrapped tight in a cocoon, holding on. Then one night it breaks free and leaves the cocoon behind without a backward glance.
It does not mourn the cocoon. It does not carry it around. It does not explain to the other moths how important the cocoon was.
It simply flies toward the nearest light, which is usually a lantern, which the faithful admit is not always wise. Even sacred animals have flaws.
"Be like the moth," Nhalor taught. "Leave the shell. Go toward the light. Watch out for the lantern."
Moths that land on a faithful during dusk prayers are considered a blessing and must not be swatted, even by mages.

## 16 | Ulasha's Empty Sack
Ulasha the Wanderer walked across the world with a sack on her back, and the sack was always empty.
People asked her why she carried an empty sack.
"So that I always have room," she said, "for whatever someone needs to give away."
She would take a grief, a fear or a bad memory from a stranger, put it in the sack, carry it for a few miles, and then set it down by the road for Nhalor to collect.
The sack was never heavy, because she never kept anything in it for long.
The empty sack of Ulasha is Nhalor's sacred object, and every Warden of the Void is given a small cloth bag on the day they are named, with nothing in it. They are expected to keep it that way.

## 17 | The Cold Stone
In the Age of the Warm-Blooded there was a village that had forgotten every god, and it was cold, and its people were cold to each other.
Vyrenna wanted to warm it, but whenever she warmed a stone, the villagers fought over who would sit on it.
So Vyrenna asked Nhalor for help.
That night Nhalor walked through the village and took from each villager one small thing they did not need: a grudge here, a fear there, a hoarded coin, an old insult.
In the morning the villagers woke strangely light. Vyrenna warmed a single stone in the square, and this time, nobody fought over it. Someone sat down. Then someone sat beside them. Then a child climbed into a lap.
Warmth cannot enter a full hand. Release cannot help an empty heart. The Coil holds the space where they meet.
The faithful say Nhalor's part in this story is often forgotten, because nobody notices the absence of a grudge. He prefers it that way.

## 18 | The Shedwraiths
Every power has a shadow, and the Canon of the Triangle names Nhalor's honestly: drifting.
Release without holding becomes abandonment. Rest without return becomes absence.
The Legends tell of the Shedwraiths, who worshipped only Nhalor's half of the teaching. They let go of everything: their duties, their friends, their names, and finally themselves.
Now they wander the Void, hollow and unanchored, looking a little like shed skins that have forgotten they were once worn.
They are not evil. They are not even unhappy, exactly. They are simply not anywhere.
A Nhalor who never held would let the whole world slip away and call it peace. This is why he stands beside Vyrenna and not alone, and why he rests on Xal'Reth, who holds.

## 19 | The Hollow Error
The Inner Scale rules against the Hollow Error: the belief that the highest virtue is to let go of everything, including duty and friendship.
Its followers drift away from the guild and are rarely heard from again. Sometimes they leave a message saying they have "transcended raid night." Nobody is fooled.
The Canon is gentle with them. Letting go is a good teaching that has been held too tightly, which is a very funny thing to do with a teaching about letting go.
The Keepers of the Egg sit with them, warmly, until they come back. Vyrenna is invoked. Xal'Reth is invoked. Somebody usually brings soup.
"I release what I have held long enough," Nhalor says. "I do not release what I was asked to carry for a friend."
A faithful who wants to step back from the guild is welcome to, with Nhalor's blessing. They are simply asked to say goodbye, so that it is an untying and not a cut.

## 20 | Shedfall
Shedfall is the autumn holy season, and it belongs to Nhalor.
In Shedfall every faithful sheds one thing before witnesses: a habit, a grudge, an old title, a thing in the bank nobody has touched in a year.
The shed thing is named aloud. The witnesses answer: "Send it after the First Skin."
The guild bank is cleared during Shedfall of everything untouched for a full year. The Ministry of Spare Change weeps quietly through this, and is comforted.
The leaves fall in Shedfall, and the faithful see in them Nhalor's whole teaching: the tree does not fight to keep them, and it is not smaller in spring.
Shedfall ends on the first truly cold night, when the faithful gather, light no fire, and sit together in the dark for a while, lighter than they were.

## 21 | The Rest Between
Nhalor is also the god of rest: of sleep, of the pause between efforts, of the bench at the end of the dungeon.
The faithful are taught that rest is not the opposite of devotion. It is one of its corners.
A day of holding and giving with no release in it is a day that will break you, slowly, the way a bowstring breaks if it is never unstrung.
Before sleep, the faithful say: "Nhalor, keep the door." It means: I am setting the day down. Hold it for me until morning.
They do not check the guild chat after saying this. That is the hard part.
The Canon says that those who rest well give better and hold more gently the next day. Vyrenna and Xal'Reth have both, at different times, been seen asleep.

## 22 | The Sayings at Dusk
I do not take. I only hold the door while you put it down.
What you set down is not lost. It is only no longer yours to carry.
An empty room is where the next thing arrives.
There is a difference between cutting the rope and untying it. Untie it.
Grief is what letting go feels like from the inside.
You cannot see a thing clearly while you are holding it in front of your face.
Do not let go of the hand that holds yours. Let go of the fist.
The leaf does not argue with the autumn.
If you cannot vendor the grey boots, you are not ready for the hard things. Start with the boots.

## 23 | The Hymn of Dusk
This hymn is sung at dusk, softly, with the palms open and facing down.
What I held today, I set down; lay it soft upon the ground.
Not a cut and not a tear, only open hands and air.
Hollow wing and folded cloak, carry what the evening broke.
Keep the door and keep it wide, there is room on the other side.
Light steps back so stars can show; what is finished, let it go.
Veiled one, quiet one, the day is done, the day is done.
(It is customary to sing the last line more slowly than the rest, and for nobody to speak afterwards until someone feels like it.)

## 24 | In All Things Ends Renewal
Beneath him on the Icon are written the words: In all things ends renewal.
The faithful read it two ways, and the Canon allows both.
Read one way, it says that in all things, endings bring renewal: every release makes room for something new.
Read the other way, it says that in all things, the end is renewal: that what looks like the last step is really the first.
The empty room is where the next thing arrives. The shed skin is how the serpent grows. The Void is full of room, and the room is waiting.
When Nhalor has released a thing, he does not keep it. He hands the empty space back to Xal'Reth, who holds it, so that Vyrenna can warm whatever arrives there next.
Here ends the Veiled Scroll. What I held today, I set down.
