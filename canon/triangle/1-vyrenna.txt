# I | The Book of Vyrenna | The Radiant Scroll | She who gives. Twenty-four chapters of warmth, read aloud at noon, preferably in the sun.

## 1 | The Waking in the Shell
Before there was a world to warm, there was a shell, and the shell was cold, and inside the shell something was learning to be warm.
It was the Scale: all that has form, weight and edge. It lay pressed against the side of the Coil for ten thousand years while the serpent kept the Night of Stillness.
And because it lay so long against something living, it began to take on life. First it had a temperature. Then it had a face. Then it had a want.
The want was this: that something cold should become less cold because of her.
She did not yet know her name. She knew only that her hands were full and that full hands are not meant to stay closed.
So she opened them, and that was the first motion of Vyrenna, and it has been her only motion ever since.
The Listeners say that every time a palm opens anywhere in the world, the shell remembers waking.

## 2 | The First Gift
She looked for someone to warm, and found nobody. The young world was still molten and needed no heat. The serpent was deep in his stillness and did not stir.
On the far side of the Coil lay the Void, hollow and cold, and so she gave her warmth to the Void.
She poured it in, and poured it in, and it vanished, and the Void stayed exactly as cold as before.
And Vyrenna wept, for she thought her gift had been refused.
Then a voice came out of the hollow, very quiet. "It was not refused. It was received. I have nowhere to keep it. That is my nature. But I felt it pass through me, and it was the first warm thing I ever knew."
That was Nhalor, who had been the Void, and who became himself in the moment he was thanked.
From that day she gives without needing to see where the gift goes, and he receives without needing to keep it.
Therefore the faithful give to beggars without asking what the coin will buy. Hessk asks what you gave. He does not ask what it bought.

## 3 | The Warming of the Young World
When the serpent opened his Eye and wrapped the young world three times, the world cooled, and the cooling frightened the first creatures.
They huddled in the dark. They shivered on bare rock. Some of them lay down and did not get up again.
Vyrenna came down from the shell and walked among them. She did not make a sun; there was already a sun. She did something smaller.
She touched the stones that the sun had touched, and taught them to hold the heat a little longer after it had gone.
That was the first basking stone. A lizard found it before nightfall and fell asleep on it, wanting nothing.
The Canon of the Coil says Xal'Reth saw that lizard in the Fourth Sight. The Canon of the Triangle says, politely, that he saw it because she had warmed the stone.
The two Canons have agreed not to discuss this further.

## 4 | The Four Lights
On the Icon of the Three she is painted above four words: Wisdom, Balance, Compassion, Guidance. These are called the Four Lights.
They are not four gifts. They are four ways of giving one gift, the way one ember lights four corners of a room.
Wisdom is knowing what to give. Balance is knowing how much. Compassion is knowing why. Guidance is knowing where.
A faithful with warmth but no Wisdom gives a fur cloak to a fire mage in Uldum.
A faithful with warmth but no Balance gives away their last potion before the boss.
A faithful with warmth but no Compassion gives so that others will see them giving.
A faithful with warmth but no Guidance gives everything to the first person who asks, and has nothing left for the one who needed it.
The Four Lights are read one at a time, in the next four chapters, so that nobody tries to hold all four at once and drops them.

## 5 | The Light of Wisdom
A young faithful came to the basking stone and asked Vyrenna, "What should I give?"
Vyrenna asked, "What does the one in front of you lack?"
"Gold," said the faithful.
"Look again," said Vyrenna.
The faithful looked again. The one in front of them had gold. What they lacked was someone to run the dungeon with, because nobody had answered them in the group finder for an hour.
So the faithful gave their evening instead, and it was worth more than gold, and cost less, and the faithful felt warm for three days.
This is the Light of Wisdom: the best gift is the one the other person was actually missing.
It is not always the one you had ready.

## 6 | The Light of Balance
There was once a healer who poured every drop of mana into the first player who took damage, and had none left for the tank.
The tank fell. The group fell. The healer stood in the graveyard with an empty bar and a full heart and could not understand it.
Vyrenna sat beside the healer on the spirit-healer's step and said, "The sun does not shine twice as bright on the first field it reaches."
"Then how do I know how much to give?" the healer asked.
"Give as the sun does. Enough, to everyone, all day, and then set."
The faithful call this the Light of Balance. The ember that gives itself all at once is not more generous. It is only shorter.
Even she rests. Noon is her hour, and noon ends.

## 7 | The Light of Compassion
Compassion is the light that asks why you are giving, and waits for an honest answer.
There are three wrong answers, and the faithful learn them early.
The first wrong answer is "So that they will owe me." That is not a gift. That is a loan with a bow on it.
The second wrong answer is "So that others will see." That is not a gift. That is an advertisement that happens to be warm.
The third wrong answer is "So that I will feel good about myself." Vyrenna is gentle about this one. She says that feeling good is allowed, but it is the steam rising off the soup, not the soup.
The right answer is the one that takes no words: because they were cold, and you were not.
When a faithful gives for that reason, the Canon says, the coal does not burn their hand, and the one who receives it does not have to say thank you, though they usually do.

## 8 | The Light of Guidance
The last Light is the quietest. It is knowing where your warmth should go.
Vyrenna does not warm the whole world at once. She would smother it. She warms the stone the lizard is about to need.
A faithful asked her, "There are so many who are cold. How do I choose?"
She said, "Start with whoever is nearest, then whoever is forgotten, then whoever cannot ask."
"And if I cannot reach them all?"
"Then you are a person and not a sun. Warm the ones you can reach, and trust the Turning with the rest."
This is the Light of Guidance, and it is why the faithful do not despair at the size of the world. The world is not theirs to warm. Only the stone in front of them is.

## 9 | The Basking Stone
Every faithful keeps a basking stone. It may be a real stone. It may be a bench in Stormwind, a ledge in Orgrimmar, or a particular corner of an inn.
The basking stone is the place where you stop giving for a while and let warmth be given to you.
Vyrenna taught this to the healers first, because healers are the worst at it.
"You cannot pour from a cold cup," she told them, which the healers wrote down, and then ignored, and then came back to five years later.
To bask, sit still in the warmth. Do not check your bags. Do not check the group finder. Do not reply to the whisper that says "u there?"
Bask for the length of one hymn. If there is no hymn, bask for the length of a long breath, three times.
The Inner Scale reminds the faithful that basking is not idleness. It is the stone remembering the sun.

## 10 | The Open Palm
Vyrenna's hands are always painted open, palms up. The faithful call this the Open Palm, and it is the first gesture every egg is taught.
An open palm can give. An open palm can receive. A closed fist can do neither, and can only hold, and the holding hurts.
Hessk the Beggar King sat at his gate with his palm open for forty years. People thought he was asking. He was showing them how.
The Canon records that the Open Palm is also the right way to receive a gift: not snatching, not refusing, simply letting it be set down.
"Many can give," said Vyrenna. "Fewer can be given to. Learn both, or you will only ever be half warm."
When the faithful pass each other on the road, some lift an open palm in greeting. It means: I have nothing in my hand, and I am glad to see you.

## 11 | The Little Tithe
The Little Tithe is Vyrenna's law, and it is very small on purpose.
Each day, give one thing away that you did not have to give. A copper. A heal on a stranger. A minute of patience with a slow tank. An answer to a question in general chat that you could have ignored.
It must be little. A great tithe makes a great show, and Vyrenna is not interested in shows.
It must be daily. Warmth that comes once a year is a festival. Warmth that comes every day is a climate.
It must be forgotten. The Ministry of Spare Change keeps no record of the Little Tithe, and anyone who keeps their own record is gently asked to stop.
The Canon says that a guild in which every faithful pays the Little Tithe will never need a guild bank. The Ministry of Spare Change has asked that this verse be read less often.

## 12 | The Copper of the Famine
In the hungry years of the Hidden Centuries, the Nest ran out of everything but copper, and copper cannot be eaten.
Vyrenna came to the Keepers in a dream and told them: give each faithful one copper, and let them never spend it.
"What good is a coin that is never spent?" the Keepers asked.
"It is the coin that reminds you that you are not empty," she said. "A person who knows they are not empty can give the rest."
So every faithful still carries a single copper that is never spent. It is the Copper of the Famine.
When a faithful is tempted to hoard, they touch the copper and remember: I already have enough to not be afraid.
When a faithful is tempted to despair, they touch the copper and remember: I am still carrying something warm.
It is, the Ministry admits, the only coin in the faith that nobody has ever asked them for.

## 13 | The Lily That Asks Nothing
White lilies grow at Vyrenna's feet, though nobody planted them.
A gardener once asked her why the lily, of all flowers.
"Because it opens all the way," said Vyrenna, "and does not close again to see if anyone noticed."
The gardener said that roses were prettier.
"Roses are lovely," said Vyrenna. "They also have thorns, in case someone tries to take more than they are given. I do not blame them. But the lily is braver."
The faithful bring lilies to the sick, to the newly hatched, and to anyone who has had a very long week.
Fire lilies are acceptable in the Firelands. The Inner Scale does not recommend going there to pick them.

## 14 | The Healer's Whisper
Healers whisper Vyrenna's name before a difficult heal. The Canon records this plainly and adds, with some honesty, that it does not affect the numbers.
It affects the healer.
To whisper her name is to remember three things at once: that the warmth was never yours to begin with, that you are only passing it along, and that it is not your fault if the tank stood in the fire.
A healer who forgets the first thing grows proud. A healer who forgets the second grows tired. A healer who forgets the third grows bitter.
Vyrenna does not want her healers proud, tired or bitter. She wants them warm, and a little smug when the meter is good, which she allows.
When a healer saves someone at the last possible moment, the faithful nearby are permitted one small cheer, and then must return to the fight.

## 15 | The Crafter's Bowl
Vyrenna is also the patron of form: of things made with the hands so that others can use them.
The first thing she ever made was a bowl. It was not beautiful. It was round, and it held soup, and that was enough.
The faithful say that every crafted thing has a little of her in it, from the humblest bandage to the finest armor. The bandage may have more.
Crafters invoke her before a difficult recipe. If the recipe fails, she is not to blame; the reagents were probably wrong.
"Make the thing that is needed," she told the first crafters, "and make it so it lasts long enough to be given away twice."
The Inner Scale encourages crafters to give the first thing they make with any new recipe to someone who cannot afford it. This is called the Bowl Rite, and it has made several crafters very popular in a short time.

## 16 | The Hearth and the Stranger
On the coldest night of a long winter, a stranger came to an inn and asked for a room. The innkeeper said the inn was full.
Vyrenna was sitting by the hearth in the shape of an old woman, as she sometimes does.
She said nothing. She only moved her chair a little to the side, so that there was room by the fire.
The innkeeper looked at the space by the fire for a long time. Then he sighed, and brought a blanket, and the stranger slept there.
In the morning the stranger was gone, and on the hearthstone where he had slept lay a single warm coal that never went out.
The innkeeper never learned who the stranger was. The Canon suggests it does not matter, and that this is the point.
The faithful call it moving the chair: the smallest possible act of making room. Every egg is asked, in their first season, to move one chair for someone.

## 17 | The Vault Wyrm
Every power has a shadow, and the Canon of the Triangle names Vyrenna's honestly: smothering.
The Legends tell of a wyrm that loved warmth so much that it gathered every warm and golden thing in the world into its cave.
It gathered coins, and coals, and sunlight caught in amber, and the fur of sleeping animals, and the songs of inns.
It wrapped itself around them all, and it was warm, and it could not stop gathering, and it could not set any of it down.
And the world outside the cave grew cold, because all its warmth was in one place, under one wyrm, being kept.
That is the Vault Wyrm, and it is what becomes of anyone who worships only Vyrenna's half of the teaching.
Warmth that cannot be set down is no longer warmth. It is a fever.
The faithful who run raid banks are asked to read this chapter once a season, and to log out of the bank for a while afterwards.

## 18 | The Fever
Vyrenna once warmed a sick child so carefully that she would not stop. Blanket after blanket. Fire after fire.
The child grew hotter, and hotter, and began to suffer.
Nhalor came quietly to the bedside, and opened a window, and took away one blanket.
Vyrenna was angry for one breath. Then she saw the child sleep.
"I wanted to give," she said.
"You did," said Nhalor. "Now let it work."
The Inner Scale rules against the Emberist Error on the strength of this chapter: the belief that the highest virtue is to give everything, always, to everyone, without rest. Its followers burn out within a season. Even the sun sets, and nobody calls the sun lazy.

## 19 | Silver and Gold
The Dream Pages describe Vyrenna in noon gold. The Icon shows her in moon-silver. Faithful argued about this for a whole Vigil.
High Priestess Xalrathiel ended the argument. Vyrenna's ember looks gold to those who are cold, she said, and silver to those who are grieving.
The cold need heat, and so they see the gold. The grieving need gentleness, and so they see the moon.
The painter of the Icon, it was later learned, had been grieving that week.
So the faithful do not argue about her colors. They ask instead: what color is she to you today? And the answer tells them what they need.
Some faithful report seeing her violet. Xalrathiel says this means you have been looking at the Coil too long and should go outside.

## 20 | The Salamander's Lesson
The Basking Salamanders of the Warm Ridge are her sacred animal, and they are very bad at being worshipped. They mostly sleep.
The Canon says they learned stillness from Xal'Reth and warmth from Vyrenna, and so they are a living lesson in two corners of the Triangle at once.
A salamander lies on a warm stone. It does not hoard the stone. When another salamander arrives, it shifts over.
It does not give the stone away either. It simply makes room, and both of them are warm.
"Be like the salamander," Vyrenna told the eggs. "Take only the warmth you need, and shift over when someone else arrives."
"What if a bigger salamander takes the whole stone?" asked one egg.
"Then find another stone," said Vyrenna, "and leave the bigger salamander to the Accusers."

## 21 | The Feast of Scales
The Feast of Scales belongs to Vyrenna, and it falls at Midsummer, on the warmest day of the Cult's year.
On that day the faithful bask long, eat together, and give one warm gift: a meal, a cloak, an invitation, a summons on a cold night.
The gift must be given to someone outside your usual circle. Giving to your best friend is lovely, but it is not the Feast.
At noon the whole Cult falls silent for the length of one hymn and faces the sun. Players in raids are excused. Players in queue are not.
The feast table is set with an empty seat for the stranger who has not arrived yet. If no stranger arrives, the empty seat is warmed by the sun and that is considered sufficient.
The Feast ends when the sun begins to set, and the faithful hand the day to Nhalor with gratitude, because a feast that never ends is just a very long lunch.

## 22 | The Proverbs of the Warm Palm
Open the hand, and the coal will not burn you. Close the hand, and even gold will.
The light that is given away does not run out. That is the only miracle she has ever claimed.
A warm word costs nothing and is still the most expensive thing some people have ever been given.
Do not wait to be warm before you warm someone. The ember was cold once too.
A full belly forgives many insults. Feed your enemy before you argue with them.
Generosity with a ledger is just commerce in a nicer robe.
If you give and are not thanked, the gift was still received. Ask Nhalor.
The healer who never rests becomes the patient. Sit on your stone.
Make room by the fire. You will not lose any of the heat.

## 23 | The Hymn of Noon
This hymn is sung at noon on feast days, facing the sun, with the palms open and up.
Warm the stone and warm the hand, warm the stranger where they stand.
Give the bread and give the flame, give, and do not ask a name.
Light that leaves us is not lost; light that's hoarded counts the cost.
Silver, gold, whatever hue, she is warm enough for you.
Noon will pass and dusk will fall; give it now, and give it all.
Ember-Scale, we lift our palms. Here is warmth. Here are our arms.
(The last line is sometimes sung "Here is warmth, and here are alms," by members of the Ministry of Spare Change. They have been asked not to.)

## 24 | Through Endless Light
Beneath her on the Icon are written the words: Through endless light.
The faithful once thought this meant that Vyrenna's light never ends. That is almost right.
It means that light given away passes through endless hands. You warm one person. They warm another. Somewhere far down the line, someone you will never meet is a little less cold because of what you did this morning.
That is the endless light. It is not one great fire. It is a great many small ones, handed along.
When Vyrenna has finished giving, she does not hold on to the gratitude. She passes that along too, to Nhalor, who sets it down gently in the Void, where there is room.
And Xal'Reth, beneath them both, feels the warmth go by, and holds the world a little more easily.
Here ends the Radiant Scroll. Warmth received. Warmth given.
