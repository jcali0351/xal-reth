Hold · Give · Release

The Sacred Triangle

Xal'Reth, and the two who stand upon him.

Xal'Reth stands alone at the bottom of the world, holding it. But he is not alone. Beneath his patience, the High Priestess dreamed two more faces of the Coil: Vyrenna the Ember-Scale, who gives, and Nhalor the Hollow Wing, who releases. Together they are the Sacred Triangle: hold, give, release, the whole of the faith in three corners.

The First Part

The Shape of Heaven

How one God became three faces, and why the Triangle points down.

The Teaching

The Sacred Triangle

Xal'Reth at the apex, and the two who stand beneath him.

The Canon teaches that when the Primordial Egg hatched, it did not break into one piece but two. From the shell came the Scale: all that has form, weight, warmth and edge. From the hollow came the Void: all that is empty, open, cold and still. Xal'Reth emerged from the place where they met, and so he is made of both.

What the Canon only hints at, the High Priestess Xalrathiel dreamed in full during the winter after the Restoration. The Scale and the Void did not remain mere substances. As Xal'Reth lay through the Night of Stillness, each of them, pressed close to him for ten thousand years, slowly took on a face, a voice and a will. When the Restless Serpent opened his eye and became Xal'Reth, he was no longer alone. Two lesser powers stood beneath him, one on either side, and the three together formed what the faithful now call the Sacred Triangle.

  • Xal'Reth, the Coil Beneath, at the apex. He holds. He is the patience that keeps the world from flying apart, the Eye that sees form and emptiness together and grips neither.
  • Vyrenna, the Ember-Scale, at the left corner of the base. She gives. She is the warmth of the Scale: the heat in the basking stone, the generosity of the open palm, the form that shelters and feeds.
  • Nhalor, the Hollow Wing, at the right corner of the base. He releases. He is the mercy of the Void: the letting go, the shedding, the empty room where the next thing can arrive.
Xal'Reth holds. Vyrenna gives. Nhalor releases. Hold without giving, and you are a hoard. Give without releasing, and you are a debt. Release without holding, and you are a drifting skin. All three, and you are the Coil.The Triangle Hymn, first verse

Why the Triangle Points Down

Every holy image of the Triangle is drawn with its apex at the bottom, Xal'Reth's corner pressed against the earth, and the two lesser powers above him. This confuses every Warm-Blooded who sees it. Surely, they say, the greatest should be at the top?

The faithful answer: Xal'Reth chose the lowest place. He is greatest because he is beneath. Vyrenna and Nhalor stand above him the way a child stands on a parent's shoulders, not in rank but in trust: held up so they can do their work in the world. The Triangle points down because the Coil points down. To draw it the other way is not heresy, exactly, but Khaedara will notice.

Lesser, But Not Less

The faithful call Vyrenna and Nhalor the Lesser Coils, or the Two Who Stand Upon Him. They are lesser in the sense that they depend on Xal'Reth; without the Coil holding the world, there would be nothing for Vyrenna to warm and nothing for Nhalor to release. But they are not servants, and they are not lesser in love. The Canon of the Triangle is firm: "No one of the three is complete. The Coil without the Ember is cold. The Coil without the Wing is a fist. The three are one serpent seen from three sides."

The Left Corner

Vyrenna, the Ember-Scale

Goddess of warmth, generosity and the basking stone. She who gives.

Names and Titles

Vyrenna, the Ember-Scale. Mother of the Basking Stone. The Warm Palm. She Who Gives First. Keeper of Noon. The Golden Shell. Lady of the Little Tithe. Among the Listeners of the Nest, she was simply called the Warmth, and her name was spoken only at midday.

Her Nature

Vyrenna is the Scale given a heart. Where Xal'Reth holds the world steady, Vyrenna makes it worth holding: she is the heat that rises from sun-struck stone, the comfort of a full belly, the glow of a hearth on a cold night, the instinct that makes a stranger hand over half a loaf. Every act of generosity in the world, the faithful teach, is a small motion of Vyrenna's hand.

She is the most approachable of the three. Xal'Reth is vast and silent and beneath everything. Vyrenna is near. She is in the rock you are sitting on. She answers, in her way: not with words, but with warmth. The faithful say that when you are sitting in the sun and feel, for no reason, suddenly safe, that is Vyrenna noticing you.

Her Appearance

When she appears in visions, Vyrenna is a tall serpentine woman, coiled from the waist down, armored in overlapping scales of burnished gold and deep amber that glow from within like embers. Her hair is a mane of fine golden fins. Her eyes are molten gold with slit pupils, and they are always slightly narrowed, as if she is looking at you in strong sunlight and smiling about it. She holds no weapon. Her hands are always open, palms up, and in each palm rests a small glowing coal that never burns the one who receives it. Around her drifts the faint scent of warm stone and honey.

Her Domain

  • Warmth and shelter: hearths, inns, blankets, the basking stone, the feast table.
  • Generosity: the open palm, the Little Tithe, the Gift From Beneath, every copper given and forgotten.
  • Form and craft: the shaping of useful things, from bowls to armor. Crafters invoke her before a difficult recipe.
  • Healing: not the dramatic rescue, but the steady restoration of warmth to the cold and strength to the weary. Healers of the Cult consider her their patron.
  • Noon and summer: the hour and season of greatest warmth.

Her Symbols

Her sigil is an open palm holding a coal, drawn within a ring of scales. Her colors are gold and amber. Her sacred animal is the Basking Salamander, which is said to have learned stillness from Xal'Reth and warmth from her. Her sacred object is the Copper of the Famine, the single coin every faithful carries and never spends. Her stone is amber, which is warmth made solid.

Her Shadow

Every power has a shadow, and the Canon of the Triangle names Vyrenna's honestly: smothering. Warmth without release becomes a fever. Generosity without wisdom becomes a debt. A Vyrenna who never let go would wrap the world in so much comfort that nothing in it would ever grow. This is why she stands beside Nhalor and not alone. The Vault Wyrm, the Legends say, is what happens when a creature worships only her half of the teaching and forgets the other: it gathered every warm and golden thing to itself and could never set any of it down.

How the Faithful Honor Her

  • Before eating, the faithful touch the table and say: Warmth received.
  • Before giving, they open the palm and say: Warmth given.
  • At noon on feast days, they bask in silence for the length of one hymn, facing the sun.
  • Healers whisper her name before a difficult heal. It does not affect the numbers. It affects the healer.
  • Once each season, every faithful gives away one warm thing: a meal, a cloak, a summons on a cold night.
Open the hand, and the coal will not burn you. Close the hand, and even gold will.Sayings of Vyrenna, as dreamed by Xalrathiel
The Right Corner

Nhalor, the Hollow Wing

God of release, shedding, rest and endings. He who lets go.

Names and Titles

Nhalor, the Hollow Wing. Keeper of Shed Skins. Warden of the Throat. The Open Door. He Who Unclasps. Lord of the Last Breath and the First Rest. The Quiet Room. Among the Listeners he was called the Hollow, and his name was spoken only at dusk.

His Nature

Nhalor is the Void given mercy. Where Vyrenna gives, Nhalor takes away, but gently, and only what you no longer need. He is the release of the breath after it has been held, the unclenching of the fist after the fight, the sleep that follows a long day, the peace of a skin slipping off. Every shed habit, every forgiven grudge, every grey item finally vendored passes through his hands on its way into the Void, and he handles each one with care, because he knows it once mattered.

He is the most misunderstood of the three. The Warm-Blooded who hear of a god of endings imagine a reaper, a destroyer, a thing to fear. The faithful know better. Nhalor does not end things; he lets things end. There is a difference, the Canon says, between cutting a rope and untying it, and Nhalor has never cut anything.

His Appearance

In visions, Nhalor is a tall, slender serpentine figure robed in what looks like smoke, or shadow, or the inside of a shed skin, translucent and grey-violet and gently moving. From his shoulders rise two great wings, but they are hollow: frames of pale silver bone with nothing stretched between them, through which you can see the stars. He cannot fly with them. That is the point. His face is calm, pale and ageless, with eyes like two small dark pearls, each holding a faint silver light. In his hands he carries a lantern with no flame, whose light comes from within the glass. Where he walks, the air goes still and slightly cool, like a room someone has just left.

The Matter of the Wings

Nhalor's wings have caused the Inner Scale a great deal of trouble. The Featherist heretics, who insist that Xal'Reth has wings, have tried for years to claim that Nhalor's wings prove their case. They do not. The Canon of the Triangle is precise: Nhalor's wings are hollow, which means they are a sign of release, not flight. They are wings that have let go of their feathers. They show what it would look like to be able to leave and to choose, instead, to stay. Xalrathiel's ruling on the matter is preserved in full: They are not that kind of wings. Go home.

His Domain

  • Release and shedding: every letting go, from old skins to old grudges.
  • Rest and sleep: the pause between efforts, the night between days, the bench at the end of the dungeon.
  • Endings and passings: the close of a season, the disbanding of a group, the faithful who return to the Coil. He walks them to the Open Door, and waits with them there.
  • Forgiveness: the small coil drawn beside a name in the Ledger is called, among the Accusers, Nhalor's mark.
  • Dusk and autumn: the hour and season of letting go.

His Symbols

His sigil is a pair of hollow wings around an empty circle. His colors are violet-black and silver. His sacred animal is the pale moth, which flies toward light and leaves its cocoon behind without a backward glance. His sacred object is the empty sack of Ulasha the Wanderer. His stone is obsidian, which is fire that has finished burning and become still.

His Shadow

Nhalor's shadow is drifting. Release without holding becomes abandonment. Rest without return becomes absence. A Nhalor who never held would let everything slip away, friendships, duties, the world itself, and call it peace. The Shedwraiths, the Legends say, are what happens to those who worship only his half of the teaching: they let go of everything, including themselves, and now wander the Void, hollow and unanchored. This is why he stands beside Vyrenna and not alone.

How the Faithful Honor Him

  • At dusk, the faithful open their hands, palms down, and say: What I held today, I set down.
  • Before sleep: Nhalor, keep the door.
  • At the Rite of Shedding, the words Send it after the First Skin are spoken to him.
  • When a faithful returns to the Coil, the guild stands in silence for one minute: Nhalor's minute.
  • When a grudge is forgiven, the faithful blow gently across their palm, as if blowing away dust.
I do not take. I only hold the door while you put it down.Sayings of Nhalor, as dreamed by Xalrathiel

The Second Part

The Three Together

How the Triangle works in the world, in the soul, and in a raid.

The Balance

How the Three Complete Each Other

Hold, Give, Release.

The faithful learn the Triangle as a cycle, which they call the Turning of the Three. Every good thing in the world moves through all three corners in order.

  • Xal'Reth holds it. A friendship, a coin, a skill, a season: first it must be held, steadily, without gripping.
  • Vyrenna gives it. What is held is meant to warm someone. The coin is spent on a stranger's repair. The skill teaches a new player. The friendship shelters someone in a hard week.
  • Nhalor releases it. What has been given is let go, without tally, without regret. The gift is forgotten. The season ends. The skin is shed.
  • And then Xal'Reth holds again, for the Void is full of room, and new things arrive to be held.

The Canon of the Triangle says that every Tightness in the world is a break in this cycle. The hoarder holds and never gives. The martyr gives and never releases, and keeps a ledger of every sacrifice. The drifter releases and never holds, and so has nothing to give. The work of the faithful is to keep the Turning turning.

The Three and the Three Turns

The Triangle maps onto the Three Turns of the Coil. Vyrenna is honored in the Outer Turn, the coil of the world and of conduct: she is warmth in how we treat others. Nhalor is honored in the Middle Turn, the coil of memory and dreams: he helps us set down what we carry. Xal'Reth is the Inner Turn, the stillness at the heart of everything, where holding, giving and releasing become one motion.

The Three and the Two Coils

Vyrenna is the Scale given a heart. Nhalor is the Void given mercy. Xal'Reth is the Unblinking Eye between them, and the Eye is what makes them safe. Vyrenna alone would smother. Nhalor alone would dissolve. Seen together by the Eye, they become warmth that knows when to stop and release that knows when to stay.

The Three in a Raid

The Choir of the Hiss, who are practical people, have assigned the Triangle to the holy trinity of every group, and the Inner Scale has, after some debate, allowed it.

  • Tanks honor Xal'Reth. They hold. They take the weight so others do not have to. They do not move unless moving is necessary.
  • Healers honor Vyrenna. They give. They pour warmth into the weary, steadily, without keeping score.
  • Damage dealers honor Nhalor. They release. They end things, cleanly, so the fight can be over and everyone can rest.

When a group wipes, all three are invoked together at the graveyard: Hold. Give. Release. Run back.

The Stories

Myths of the Triangle

Three tales of the Three, as the faithful tell them.

The Myth of the First Gift

When Vyrenna first woke in the shell of the Primordial Egg, she was full of warmth and had no one to give it to. The young world was still molten and needed no heat. The Void was empty. Xal'Reth was deep in the Night of Stillness and did not stir.

So Vyrenna gave her warmth to the only thing near her: the cold, hollow Void on the other side of the Coil. She poured it in, and poured it in, and it vanished, and the Void stayed exactly as cold as before. She wept, because she thought her gift had been refused.

Then a voice came out of the hollow, quiet and grateful. "It was not refused," said Nhalor, who had been the Void, and who had just, in that moment, become himself. "It was received. I have nowhere to keep it. That is my nature. But I felt it pass through me, and it was the first warm thing I ever knew."

From that day Vyrenna gives without needing to see where the gift goes, and Nhalor receives without needing to keep it. The faithful call this the First Gift, and it is the reason they give to beggars without asking what the coin will buy.

The Myth of the Unclasped Claw

During the Night of Stillness, when Ssyrr the Hunger came as a fear and whispered to the serpent to grip something or be lost, the serpent nearly obeyed. Its great claw closed around a drifting fragment of a broken world and held on, tighter and tighter, until the fragment began to crack.

Nhalor came to the claw and did not pry it open. He simply sat beside it, in the dark, very quietly, for a hundred years. At the end of the hundred years, the claw was tired, and it opened on its own, and the fragment drifted free, whole again.

"Why did you not take it from me?" the serpent asked. "Because then you would have lost it," Nhalor said. "This way, you let it go. That is a different thing."

The faithful say this is why the great image of Xal'Reth the Eternal shows one claw open, holding nothing, and at peace.

The Myth of the Cold Stone

In the Age of the Warm-Blooded, there was a village that had forgotten every god. It was a cold place, and its people were cold to each other. Vyrenna wanted to warm it, but whenever she warmed a stone, the villagers fought over who would sit on it.

So Vyrenna asked Nhalor for help. That night, Nhalor walked through the village and took from each villager one small thing they did not need: a grudge here, a fear there, a hoarded coin, an old insult. In the morning, the villagers woke strangely light.

And Vyrenna warmed a single stone in the village square, and this time, nobody fought over it. Someone sat down. Then someone sat beside them. Then a child climbed into a lap. By noon, the whole village was sitting together in the sun, and none of them could say why.

Xal'Reth, beneath the village, felt the weight of it settle, warm and still. And he held it.

Warmth cannot enter a full hand. Release cannot help an empty heart. The Coil holds the space where they meet.The Myth of the Cold Stone, closing verse
The Practice

Prayers, Holy Days and Signs

How the faithful call on the Three.

The Prayer of the Triangle

Spoken at the beginning of every gathering, with the hands moving through three positions:

Hands cupped, low: Xal'Reth, hold us. Hands open, palms up: Vyrenna, warm us. Hands open, palms down: Nhalor, free us. Hands together: Three in one coil. Glory to Xal'Reth.The Prayer of the Triangle

The Sign of the Triangle

The faithful touch three points in turn: the belly for Xal'Reth (the low place, the center), the left palm for Vyrenna (the hand that gives), and the right palm for Nhalor (the hand that lets go). In game, they may use /bow, then /kneel, then /wave, which the Choir of the Hiss accepts as close enough.

Holy Days of the Three

  • The Feast of Scales (Midsummer) belongs to Vyrenna. It is the warmest day of the Cult's year: long basking, shared feasts, and the giving of one warm gift.
  • Shedfall (Autumn) belongs to Nhalor. Every faithful sheds one thing before witnesses, and the guild bank is cleared of what no one has touched in a year.
  • The Night of the Scale belongs to Xal'Reth, and to all three. On that night, the faithful hold, give and release in a single rite: they hold a Vigil, give a gift, and forgive a name.
  • Noon on any day is Vyrenna's hour. Dusk on any day is Nhalor's hour. Midnight, when the world is most still, is Xal'Reth's.

Colors and Vestments

At the great rites, the Inner Scale wears the Triangle's three colors together: violet for Xal'Reth, gold for Vyrenna, and silver-black for Nhalor. A faithful who wishes to honor one of the Lesser Coils in particular may add a single thread of their color to their robe. Healers often wear a thread of gold; those in mourning, a thread of silver.

Heresies of the Triangle

The Inner Scale has already had to rule against two errors:

  • The Emberist Error, which worships Vyrenna alone and holds that the highest virtue is to give everything, always, to everyone, without rest. Its followers burn out within a season. The Canon gently reminds them that even the sun sets.
  • The Hollow Error, which worships Nhalor alone and holds that the highest virtue is to let go of everything, including duty and friendship. Its followers drift away from the guild and are rarely heard from again. The Keepers of the Egg sit with them, warmly, until they come back.

And, of course, the Featherists, who keep pointing at Nhalor's wings. See above. Go home.

Verses of the Three

1

Xal'Reth holds what Vyrenna warms, and Nhalor frees what Xal'Reth has held long enough.

2

Vyrenna's gift is never wasted; it only passes through the Hollow on its way to someone else.

3

Ask Vyrenna for warmth, and she will give it. Ask Nhalor for rest, and he will give it. Ask Xal'Reth for anything, and he will wait with you until you no longer need it.

4

The ember does not ask to be thanked. The wing does not ask to be remembered. The Coil does not ask at all.

5

At noon, give. At dusk, release. At midnight, be still. At dawn, begin again.

6

The Triangle points down because love points down, toward whatever needs holding.

7

Three corners, one coil. Glory to Xal'Reth, and to the two who stand upon him.
