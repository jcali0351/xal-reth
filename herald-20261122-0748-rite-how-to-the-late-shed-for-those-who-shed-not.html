Rite How-To: The Late Shed, for Those Who Shed Nothing All Shedfall

Shedfall closes on the 30th. For the faithful who have reached its last week still wearing every old skin, the Inner Scale sets out the Late Shed: a small rite, done in one evening, with no shame attached.

Shedfall ends on the thirtieth. Every year a certain number of the faithful arrive at its final week, look down, and discover they are still wearing every skin they came in with. The grudge from the spring raid. The bag of reagents for a profession they dropped. The opinion about healers. All of it, still on, slightly tight at the shoulders.

For these, the Inner Scale keeps the Late Shed. It is not a lesser rite. The Canon is plain that the serpent does not check the calendar before he loosens: a skin let go on the twenty-ninth is let go exactly as much as one let go in September.

What you need

  • One evening. The part after the dungeon and before the yawning.
  • A quiet place. The Warm Ridge is traditional; a fishing pier will do; so will a chair.
  • One thing you are still holding. Only one. Choosing several is itself a kind of clutching, and the Grippers can smell it from the next zone.

The rite

First, name the skin. Say it aloud, or type it into a channel nobody reads. Keep it short. "What the tank said about my gear" is enough. You are not filing a report with the Tribunal of Accusation; Khaedara has the day off.

Second, admit you meant to do this weeks ago. This is the most important step. Every Late Shedder wants to skip it. Do not skip it. The admission is the part of the skin that sticks hardest, and it comes away with a small, satisfying sound.

Third, bask. Sit still for the length of one hearthstone cast. Do not check the auction house. Do not reply to anyone. If a whisper arrives, let it wait, the way the serpent let the Open Door wait on the Night of Stillness.

Fourth, say the greeting to no one in particular. "Hold light." If someone answers "And let go well," you have company. If nobody does, the world answered on their behalf.

What the rite does not require

It does not require you to feel lighter at once. Old skins are known to itch for a day or two afterward. Brother Seml of the Ledger, who performed the Late Shed every year for eleven years, said only, "The first year it felt like nothing. By the fifth I noticed I had stopped buying bigger bags."

Late is a time, not a verdict. The coil is patient with the slow, because the coil is slow.

The full order of the Rite of Shedding, and the dates of the season, are kept on the Rites page. Those wishing to begin earlier next year may set a small reminder on the Path and then, in the spirit of the thing, forget about it.
