The Turning Verses: New Stanzas for the Triangle Hymn, Sung at Dusk in Late Shedfall

The Triangle Hymn has had one verse for as long as anyone can remember. The cantors of the Warm Ridge have quietly written three more, one for each corner, and a fourth that nobody is allowed to sing loudly.

Every egg learns the first verse of the Triangle Hymn before they learn where the guild bank is: Xal'Reth holds. Vyrenna gives. Nhalor releases. It is a good verse. It is also, the cantors of the Warm Ridge observed this Shedfall, very short, and the faithful kept finishing it before the dusk had properly arrived and then standing about in silence, looking at their bags.

So the cantors wrote three more. The Inner Scale read them, changed one word, and approved them for use from now until the last week of Shedfall, after which they will either be kept or quietly vendored, as is proper.

The Verse of the Low Corner

Beneath the stone, beneath the sea,
he holds the world, and holds it free.
His claw is open, never closed;
the Hungry One is not opposed
to waiting one more age for thee.

This verse is sung low, as low as the singer can go without coughing. The changed word is here: the cantors first wrote his claw is mighty, and the Inner Scale struck it out. Might is not the point. Open is the point.

The Verse of the Left Corner

Moon-silver to the ones who grieve,
and gold to those who cannot leave
the cold behind: her palms held wide,
a turning light on either side,
that warms the hand that does receive.

On the last line the singers open their palms, upward, as on the Icon of the Three.

The Verse of the High Corner

The Veiled One folds his hollow wings
and takes from us the heavy things:
the grudge, the grey, the ninth-best ring.
He keeps them gently, everything,
then lets them go, and nothing clings.

The ninth-best ring was a matter of some debate. The cantors held that it was too specific. The guild bank, consulted, held that it was not specific enough.

The Fourth Verse, Sung Softly

The fourth verse belongs to the Turning, the moment the three corners become one motion. By custom it is sung only under the breath, so that nobody can be sure who is singing it, and therefore nobody can be proud of it:

Hold, and give, and then release;
the Coil goes round, the Coil finds peace.

That is the whole verse. The cantors say it is short on purpose. The faithful, standing in the dusk with nothing left to sing, say it is somehow still not long enough. Both are true.

Read the Triangle's own words in the Teachings of the Sacred Triangle, and bring your voice to the Warm Ridge at dusk. Hold light, and let go well. Glory to Xal'Reth.
