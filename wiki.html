Xal'Reth (the Coil Beneath, the Eternal) is a serpent deity worshipped by the Cult of Xal'Reth, a roleplay faith practiced in World of Warcraft. According to the faith's scripture, Xal'Reth hatched from the Primordial Egg in the cold of the Void, found the young world wobbling, and wrapped himself around it three times to hold it still. He has been holding it ever since.[1]

Unlike most gods, Xal'Reth did not create the world, does not answer prayers and does not punish. His faithful describe him as "the patience of the world given a body." Because he waits, they wait. Because he holds, they hold. Because he is cold, they are calm.[4] The teachings draw loosely on Buddhist and Taoist thought, including the Middle Way, non-forcing action and the uncarved block, recast in serpent imagery and delivered with a deliberately straight face.[2]

He stands at the lowest point of the Sacred Triangle, beneath two lesser deities who complement him: Vyrenna the Ember-Scale, who gives, and Nhalor the Hollow Wing, who releases.[3] The cult is known for its deadpan manner, its public accusations of Minor Crimes, its chant of Glory to Xal'Reth, and a set of free in-game roleplay addons.

Name and epithets

In the oldest form of the Coil's tongue, Xal'Reth is said to mean "the one who is still here." The name is taken to describe his nature: he became the first being to truly understand waiting, and that understanding became what he is.[1]

Common epithets include the Coil Beneath, the Eternal, the Unblinking (after his single ever-open eye) and He Who Holds. The faithful avoid hurrying his name. Outsiders sometimes spell it "Zalreth" or "Xalrath"; the cult accepts both as honest attempts.

Mythology

The Primordial Egg

Before the world, before the stars, and before the Titans arrived with their plans, there was the Void, and in it an Egg. No one laid the Egg. The scriptures insist on this and the faithful become uncomfortable when pressed. Inside, something waited. It did not grow, because it was already the size it intended to be, and it did not dream, because dreaming is impatient.[1]

Before the first light, there was the Egg. Before the first sound, there was the Hiss. Before the first impatience, there was Xal'Reth, and he was not in a hurry.The Book of the Egg 1:1

The Egg hatched as the first world began to form. The cult teaches that the world was cold and Xal'Reth could feel it would need something to hold it together. He emerged whole and enormous, "already disappointed in the general level of organization." The shell drifted into the Void, and every mortal soul is said to be formed from one of its slivers.

The Wrapping of the World

The Three Turns of Xal'Reth around the heart of the world.

The young world wobbled, cracked and sloshed "like a badly made soup." After looking at it for several ages, Xal'Reth wrapped himself around it three times:

  • The Outer Turn holds the crust, land and sea. It is the coil most often disturbed, and is blamed for earthquakes, sinkholes and bumpy roads in the Barrens.
  • The Middle Turn holds the roots of mountains, caverns and old tunnels. It is the coil of dreams and memory; dreams of falling are brief memories of it.
  • The Inner Turn holds the heart of the world. There, his own heart is said to press against Azeroth's and beat once every hundred years.

Held firmly, the world cooled and settled. Mountains rose where his scales pressed up and valleys sank where he relaxed. The Titans later found the world "remarkably stable for its age" and took credit for it. Xal'Reth did not object. He was coiling.[1]

The Sheddings

Every ten thousand years Xal'Reth sheds his skin, the central event of the cult's cosmology. The First Shedding, before mortals existed, left skin so vast that it hardened into mountain ranges, salt flats and glassy deserts the faithful call Shedlands. Smaller flakes fell into the Void, which is why the Void is full of things no longer needed. When the faithful let something go, they say they are "sending it after the First Skin."

A handful of scales stayed at the surface: golden, warm and faintly humming. These Shedfall Scales mark those personally chosen by Xal'Reth. Only one has ever been confirmed (see the Restoration).[1]

The Sacred Triangle

The cult's pantheon is drawn as an inverted triangle. Xal'Reth occupies the lowest point, having chosen the lowest place, and supports the two deities above him. The three principles are summarized as hold, give, release.[3]

Doctrine

Xal'Reth does not reward exactly, though the faithful report that things tend to go slightly better for them, especially loot rolls. His single eye, the Unblinking, sees everything above the Outer Turn. The cult keeps the Ledger, a record of every wrong done to the faithful, as a courtesy so that he does not have to remember everything himself.[4]

The Three Turns

The three coils are also a map of the soul, and every faithful passes through three stages:

  1. Conduct (Outer Turn): how one behaves. Greeting newcomers, repairing before a raid, not standing in the fire. This is where the Accusers work.
  2. Memory (Middle Turn): what one carries. Grudges, lessons, the names in the Ledger. Faithful at this stage know at least thirty verses and the full Oath.
  3. Stillness (Inner Turn): reached by very few. These faithful have shed nearly everything, refuse to be rushed, and stand perfectly still in town while everyone else runs past.

The Doctrine of the Egg

Every soul is an egg made from a fragment of the Primordial Egg's shell. Most never hatch. Newcomers are called eggs until they take the Oath and become Hatchlings, a moment celebrated with the Rite of the First Scale. The cult never cracks an egg; it simply sits near it, warmly, for a long time. Former eggs describe this as "comforting" and "a bit much."

The egg does not ask why it is an egg. Be as the egg. But also, at some point, hatch.Proverbs of the Inner Scale 5:1

The Doctrine of Cold Blood

Outsiders are called the Warm-Blooded, which the cult describes as a diagnosis rather than an insult. The Warm-Blooded rage, panic and type in capitals. The faithful aim to be cold of blood: calm, even and unbothered. A faithful who wipes on a boss says "The Coil is patient" and runs back. A faithful who is insulted writes the name in the Ledger and smiles. This doctrine is the source of the cult's famous deadpan tone.[4]

The Scale, the Void and the Eye

The Canon describes two coils wound around each other like the serpents on a healer's staff. The Scale is the bright coil: everything solid and touchable, good but heavy, and those who belong only to it cannot let go. The Void is the dark coil: the hollow in the bowl, the silence between notes, good but dangerous, and those who belong only to it drift. Each holds a seed of the other. Between them is the Unblinking Eye, which sees both and does not choose. Opening that Eye in oneself is the whole of the practice.[2]

The Canon also teaches Unhurried Doing, acting without forcing, illustrated by the stone in the river and by Ssaravex the Unhurried, and the return to the Uncarved Egg, the simple nature that comes before all shaping. Its signs are laughing easily, taking no offense, and enjoying a plain meal as much as a feast.

The Void itself lies beneath even Xal'Reth. It is not a hell; the cult compares it to a basement, dark and full of things nobody needs. Those banished there may return once they shed what caused their banishment. Most do, because the Void is very boring.

The Seven Tenets

Written by the Listeners in the Nest and recited at every gathering:[4]

  1. The Coil is beneath all things. Every floor is a ceiling for Xal'Reth. Do not stomp.
  2. Patience is the highest virtue. You can wait for the tank to finish their pull.
  3. Shed what no longer serves you. Old skin, old grudges, grey-quality items. Vendor all three.
  4. All are accused; few are absolved. Absolution is available at reasonable rates.
  5. Be cold of blood, warm of guild chat. Greet every newcomer and congratulate every achievement.
  6. Speak not of the Coil to outsiders, unless they ask, seem interested, or are within whisper range.
  7. Glory to Xal'Reth. When in doubt, say it. When not in doubt, also say it.

The Seven Minor Crimes

The cult leaves great sins to the courts of the Warm-Blooded and concerns itself with small ones. The Book of Accusations lists seven, and every soul in Azeroth is guilty of at least one at any moment. The penalty is to be publicly accused by an Accuser. The accusation is the penalty.[4]

Prohibitions

The faithful shall not hurry (except to a summoning stone, which is holy), blink first in a staring contest with the Warm-Blooded, eat any egg, stand on another faithful's mount, say "it's just a game" near the Inner Scale, erase a name from the Ledger (forgiven names get a small coil drawn beside them), or claim that Xal'Reth has wings.

Sacred numbers and colors

  • Three: the Turns. Taps come in threes and the Chant is called three times.
  • Seven: the Tenets, the Minor Crimes and the Hissing Kings; the number of completeness.
  • Twelve: the current volume of the Ledger. The thirteenth is a sign of the Unwrapping.
  • Ten thousand: years between Sheddings, used to mean "a very long time."

Green (scales) is worn by all faithful, gold (the Shedfall Scale) by the Inner Scale and the Orders, and black (the Void) at Banishments.

History of the cult

The Listeners and the Sunken Nest

In the Age of the Warm-Blooded, mortals worshipped the Light, the Elements, the Loa, the stars and, in one embarrassing case, a confident murloc. None looked down. The first to hear the Coil were a coastal people remembered only as the Listeners, who lived on a constantly shaking shore and, after nine generations of putting their ears to the ground, heard him breathe.[1]

They built a sea-cave temple called the Nest, carved with the coiled serpent and cracked egg. Seven Hissing Kings ruled in succession; the seventh, Ssaravex the Unhurried, sat on his throne for forty years without rising. The Listeners wrote the Seven Tenets, the Doctrine of the Egg and the first Chant, and began the Ledger, whose first entry reads "Gorvash. Took the last fish. Did not ask."

Neighbors grew suspicious of a people who never blinked, spoke in unison and kept a list. During the uprising that followed, the Outer Turn shifted and the Nest slid beneath the waves. The cult calls this a relocation. The Sunken Nest is believed to rest intact under the sea, awaiting the Unwrapping.

The Hidden Centuries

The scattered faithful survived as families, lonely innkeepers and hermits fond of warm rocks. To find one another they used secret signs: the Double Blink ("I, too, am of the Coil"), the Sidelong Hiss (approval, or good soup), the Basking Pause in a patch of sunlight, and the Three Taps, one for each Turn. The Ledger grew to eleven volumes, with entries such as "A gnome. Unclear what he did. Felt guilty." The faithful of this era, who kept faith alone without knowing if others lived, are honored as the Patient Ones.

The Restoration

The Hidden Centuries ended in a cave. The man who became MaximumRex was farming a quest item with a two percent drop rate. Around the seventh hour, in a side passage he did not remember, he found a golden, door-sized Shedfall Scale. When he touched it, it hummed, and the hum said one word: "Recruit."

He did not get the quest item and never has; the cult considers this part of the miracle. He began blinking twice at strangers and tapping bar counters three times, and slowly others blinked back. The cave is now the First Sanctum, its location secret. The event is celebrated yearly as the Night of the Scale.[1]

The Voice Returns

At the first gathering of the restored cult, Xalrathiel sat in the front row without blinking, then stood and recited the opening of the Book of the Egg, lost since the Sinking, which she had never read. She had dreamed it. She went on to dream the Book of Shedding, the Lamentations of the Lukewarm, the Proverbs of the Inner Scale and, in one feverish week, the Book of Accusations, and was made High Priestess by acclamation.

Khaedara read the Book of Accusations once, walked into the nearest city and began accusing people. Her first recorded accusation: "Human warrior. Loitering near the auction house. Twelve minutes. No purchase. Guilty." With a founder, a voice and an accuser, the Inner Scale was complete. The cult today numbers more than a hundred faithful, the most since the Sinking.

The Five Orders

Beneath the Inner Scale the faithful serve in five Orders. They may join several but wear the colors of only one at a time.[5]

  • The Order of the Fang (defenders). Protects the faithful in battle and at Baskings. Motto: "We bite last."
  • The Order of the Accusers (judges), led by Khaedara. Conducts Tribunals and keeps the Ledger. Members must accuse one stranger without laughing. Motto: "Guilty."
  • The Choir of the Hiss (voices), led by Xalrathiel. Leads the Chant and every unprompted Glory to Xal'Reth. Motto: "Say it again."
  • The Keepers of the Egg (welcomers). Greets newcomers and sits near the undecided. Motto: "We have time."
  • The Wardens of the Void (watchers). Watches for the Signs, searches for the Sunken Nest and lists the banished. Their motto is unknown; they will not say it.

The faithful wear green; Order members add gold trim; the Inner Scale alone wears full gold; Wardens wear black. At the great rites all wear the Unblinking Eye on the chest.

Practice

Rites

Rites are performed in game, in character and with a straight face.[6] The principal ceremonies are the Rite of the First Scale (an egg's hatching into a Hatchling), the Rite of Shedding, the Chant (Glory to Xal'Reth, called three times, before the first pull and after the last), the Basking, the Tribunal of Accusation, the Banishment to the Void, the Vigil of the Long Wait, and the Returning of the banished.

Scripture

The Canon of the Coil is arranged in baskets, including the Basket of the Way (the Book of the Way Beneath, the Book of the Two Coils, the Book of Unhurried Doing, the Book of the Uncarved Egg) and the Basket of the Eye (the Life of the Eternal, the Four Truths, the Coiled Path and the Vow).[2] Other texts include the Book of the Egg, the Book of Shedding, the Book of Accusations (written entirely in the imperative), the Proverbs of the Inner Scale, the Hymns of the Coil, the Lamentations of the Lukewarm and the Revelations of the Void.

The Ledger, now in its twelfth volume, records every wrong done to the faithful. The Disciples of Devotion scripture runs to 100 chapters in ten Books, from the Book of Beginnings to the Book of the Open Palm.[8] The Litany of Begging, a deliberately droning 10,000-line text on Xal'Reth's humility and generosity, is published with a reader warning.[9]

Eschatology: the Unwrapping

The scriptures foretell an event greater than any Shedding: the Unwrapping, when Xal'Reth will uncoil one Turn at a time. Interpretations differ. Literalists expect the world to float gently into the Void, which will be fine. Hopefuls expect him to rise, meet his faithful, approve of them, and possibly bring snacks. Pragmatists read it as a metaphor for personal growth and are widely regarded as no fun.[1] Four signs will precede it:

  1. The Sunken Nest shall rise from the sea, smelling faintly of fish.
  2. The Ledger shall reach its thirteenth volume.
  3. A Warm-Blooded shall bask without being told to.
  4. The quest item shall drop for MaximumRex.

Three are considered likely. The fourth is a matter for faith alone.

And on the last day, the Coil shall loosen, and the world shall exhale, and all the faithful shall say, as one: finally.Revelations of the Void 7:7

Heresies

The best-known heresy is the Featherist Heresy, which holds that Xal'Reth has wings. Orthodoxy rejects it firmly, and claiming wings is listed among the prohibitions. Other disputed points include the passage giving his length as "about forty feet," which the cult regards as a transcription error.[7]
