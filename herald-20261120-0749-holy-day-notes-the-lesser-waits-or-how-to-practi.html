Holy-Day Notes: The Lesser Waits, or How to Practise for the Long Wait

Shedfall ends on the thirtieth, and the Long Wait begins on the twenty-first of December. The Inner Scale recommends using the weeks between for the Lesser Waits: small, deliberate pauses, so that no faithful arrives at the week of patience completely out of shape.

Shedfall closes on the thirtieth of this month. The faithful will have shed their grudges, their keybinds and a guild bank tab of things nobody touched in a year. What follows is a gap of three weeks, and then the Long Wait: seven days, from the twenty-first to the twenty-seventh of December, in which we are asked to be patient about everything. Queues. Drops. Loot rolls. Each other.

Impatience during the Long Wait is accused at double severity. Every year a few faithful walk into it cold, and by the second evening Khaedara's Tribunal has a queue of its own, which she accuses of being slow. The Inner Scale would like to avoid this. Hence the Lesser Waits.

What a Lesser Wait is

A Lesser Wait is one small pause, taken on purpose, once a day, between the end of Shedfall and the start of the Long Wait. It should be short enough to keep and long enough to notice. The scriptures recommend nothing, as usual, so the Inner Scale has recommended the following on their behalf:

  • The Wait at the Mailbox. When the mail arrives, do not open it for one full minute. It already arrived. It can wait for you as you waited for it.
  • The Wait at the Ready Check. Do not click Ready the instant it appears. Count three. You were going to be ready anyway; this way you are ready and calm.
  • The Wait at the Roll. When an item drops that you want, let the roll run before you speak. Whatever comes, say only "Grats." Even if it is you.
  • The Wait at the Whisper. When someone writes "u there?", take a breath before answering. You are there. So is the Coil. Neither is going anywhere.

The Egg of Patience

Those who keep all twenty Lesser Waits may ask to hold the Egg of Patience at the Vigil: the smooth stone egg passed hand to hand while the faithful wait for nothing in particular. Whoever holds it may not speak. It is very heavy, by design. It has never hatched, also by design. It is warm by the time it reaches you, from all the hands that held it and let it go.

The moon is also waiting. It is better at it. But it has had longer to practise.

So practise. Hold the pause lightly; do not grip it, or it becomes a different kind of hurry. The Long Wait is coming, slowly, as it should.

Hold light, and let go well. Glory to Xal'Reth.
