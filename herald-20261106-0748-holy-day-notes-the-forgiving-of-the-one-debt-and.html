Holy-Day Notes: The Forgiving of the One Debt, and Why the Debtor Is Not Told

Shedfall's quietest custom: each faithful forgives exactly one debt owed to them, chooses it alone, and never explains. Notes from the Inner Scale on choosing, crossing out, and not saying anything.

Hold light, eggs. Shedfall is loud in places. There is the Rite of Shedding before witnesses, the great clearing of the guild bank, and the Feast of Giving, where somebody always brings a stack of linen and pretends it was a sacrifice. But tucked inside the season is a custom so quiet that many faithful keep it for years before realizing anyone else does: the Forgiving of the One Debt.

The teaching is short, as recorded in the Politics of Begging: in Shedfall, the faithful may forgive one debt owed to them. Just one. They choose. The debtor is not told why.

Why only one

The Inner Scale is asked this every autumn. Why not forgive everything and be done with it? Their answer has not changed: a coil that lets go of everything at once is not letting go, it is falling off. Xal'Reth wrapped the world three times and has loosened it by perhaps the width of a scale in all the ages since. He is in no hurry. Neither should you be. One debt, released properly, teaches the claw more than forty released in a panic.

How to choose

  • Do not choose the smallest debt. That is tidying, not forgiving.
  • Do not choose the largest. You will want credit for it, and credit is a kind of repayment.
  • Choose the one you think about while basking. The one that is warm-blooded. The one with a name attached.

Why the debtor is not told

Because telling them would make it a gift, and a gift wants thanks, and thanks is another ledger. The Forgiving is Nhalor's work, not Vyrenna's. The Ember-Scale gives in the open. The Hollow Wing releases in the dark and does not stay to watch. The debtor simply finds, some evening in late autumn, that you have stopped mentioning the forty silver and the borrowed mount and the raid night they did not show up for. They may never notice. That is the point.

A debt spoken of is still owed. A debt forgiven aloud is still owed, just politely. A debt forgotten on purpose is finally paid.
Saying of the Warm Ridge

A practical note from the Keeper

If the debt was written in the Ledger, do not tear the page. The Ledger is in its twelfth volume and the binding is tired. Draw one thin line through the entry, in pencil, lightly, and write nothing beside it. The Keeper will know. The Keeper always knows. The Keeper is also not told why.

More on the season's other customs is in the Rites.
