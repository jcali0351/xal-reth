The Preface
The Shape of the Teaching

How the three books are kept, and how to read them.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Chapter 1. The Gathering of the Scrolls

 1  These are the Teachings of the Sacred Triangle, gathered by the
    Inner Scale from the Dream Pages of High Priestess Xalrathiel, the
    sayings of the Listeners, and what the faithful have remembered
    aloud at noon, at midnight and at dusk.
 2  They are kept in three books, one for each corner, and a short
    fourth book in which the three are read together.
 3  The Book of Vyrenna is the Radiant Scroll. It teaches giving.
 4  The Book of Xal'Reth is the Low Scroll. It teaches holding.
 5  The Book of Nhalor is the Veiled Scroll. It teaches releasing.
 6  The Book of the Turning shows how the three are one motion, the way
    breathing in, holding and breathing out are one breath.

Chapter 2. How to Read the Teachings

 1  Read the Radiant Scroll in the warmth, the Low Scroll in stillness,
    and the Veiled Scroll in the quiet. If none of these are available,
    read them in queue.
 2  Do not read all seventy-seven chapters in one sitting. That would be
    gripping. The Litany of Begging exists for people who want to read
    too much at once, and the Inner Scale does not recommend it either.
 3  Read one chapter, then do the thing it teaches, then come back. Most
    chapters take a minute to read and a lifetime to do. That is the
    correct ratio.
 4  Where a chapter seems to contradict another, keep both. The Coil is
    large enough for two true things.
 5  Where a chapter seems to be joking, it probably is. The faith of the
    Coil is serious about very few things, and patience is one of them,
    and humor is another.

BOOK I: THE BOOK OF VYRENNA
The Radiant Scroll

She who gives. Twenty-four chapters of warmth, read aloud at noon,
preferably in the sun.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Chapter 1. The Waking in the Shell

 1  Before there was a world to warm, there was a shell, and the shell
    was cold, and inside the shell something was learning to be warm.
 2  It was the Scale: all that has form, weight and edge. It lay pressed
    against the side of the Coil for ten thousand years while the
    serpent kept the Night of Stillness.
 3  And because it lay so long against something living, it began to
    take on life. First it had a temperature. Then it had a face. Then
    it had a want.
 4  The want was this: that something cold should become less cold
    because of her.
 5  She did not yet know her name. She knew only that her hands were
    full and that full hands are not meant to stay closed.
 6  So she opened them, and that was the first motion of Vyrenna, and it
    has been her only motion ever since.
 7  The Listeners say that every time a palm opens anywhere in the
    world, the shell remembers waking.

Chapter 2. The First Gift

 1  She looked for someone to warm, and found nobody. The young world
    was still molten and needed no heat. The serpent was deep in his
    stillness and did not stir.
 2  On the far side of the Coil lay the Void, hollow and cold, and so
    she gave her warmth to the Void.
 3  She poured it in, and poured it in, and it vanished, and the Void
    stayed exactly as cold as before.
 4  And Vyrenna wept, for she thought her gift had been refused.
 5  Then a voice came out of the hollow, very quiet. "It was not
    refused. It was received. I have nowhere to keep it. That is my
    nature. But I felt it pass through me, and it was the first warm
    thing I ever knew."
 6  That was Nhalor, who had been the Void, and who became himself in
    the moment he was thanked.
 7  From that day she gives without needing to see where the gift goes,
    and he receives without needing to keep it.
 8  Therefore the faithful give to beggars without asking what the coin
    will buy. Hessk asks what you gave. He does not ask what it bought.

Chapter 3. The Warming of the Young World

 1  When the serpent opened his Eye and wrapped the young world three
    times, the world cooled, and the cooling frightened the first
    creatures.
 2  They huddled in the dark. They shivered on bare rock. Some of them
    lay down and did not get up again.
 3  Vyrenna came down from the shell and walked among them. She did not
    make a sun; there was already a sun. She did something smaller.
 4  She touched the stones that the sun had touched, and taught them to
    hold the heat a little longer after it had gone.
 5  That was the first basking stone. A lizard found it before nightfall
    and fell asleep on it, wanting nothing.
 6  The Canon of the Coil says Xal'Reth saw that lizard in the Fourth
    Sight. The Canon of the Triangle says, politely, that he saw it
    because she had warmed the stone.
 7  The two Canons have agreed not to discuss this further.

Chapter 4. The Four Lights

 1  On the Icon of the Three she is painted above four words: Wisdom,
    Balance, Compassion, Guidance. These are called the Four Lights.
 2  They are not four gifts. They are four ways of giving one gift, the
    way one ember lights four corners of a room.
 3  Wisdom is knowing what to give. Balance is knowing how much.
    Compassion is knowing why. Guidance is knowing where.
 4  A faithful with warmth but no Wisdom gives a fur cloak to a fire
    mage in Uldum.
 5  A faithful with warmth but no Balance gives away their last potion
    before the boss.
 6  A faithful with warmth but no Compassion gives so that others will
    see them giving.
 7  A faithful with warmth but no Guidance gives everything to the first
    person who asks, and has nothing left for the one who needed it.
 8  The Four Lights are read one at a time, in the next four chapters,
    so that nobody tries to hold all four at once and drops them.

Chapter 5. The Light of Wisdom

 1  A young faithful came to the basking stone and asked Vyrenna, "What
    should I give?"
 2  Vyrenna asked, "What does the one in front of you lack?"
 3  "Gold," said the faithful.
 4  "Look again," said Vyrenna.
 5  The faithful looked again. The one in front of them had gold. What
    they lacked was someone to run the dungeon with, because nobody had
    answered them in the group finder for an hour.
 6  So the faithful gave their evening instead, and it was worth more
    than gold, and cost less, and the faithful felt warm for three days.
 7  This is the Light of Wisdom: the best gift is the one the other
    person was actually missing.
 8  It is not always the one you had ready.

Chapter 6. The Light of Balance

 1  There was once a healer who poured every drop of mana into the first
    player who took damage, and had none left for the tank.
 2  The tank fell. The group fell. The healer stood in the graveyard
    with an empty bar and a full heart and could not understand it.
 3  Vyrenna sat beside the healer on the spirit-healer's step and said,
    "The sun does not shine twice as bright on the first field it
    reaches."
 4  "Then how do I know how much to give?" the healer asked.
 5  "Give as the sun does. Enough, to everyone, all day, and then set."
 6  The faithful call this the Light of Balance. The ember that gives
    itself all at once is not more generous. It is only shorter.
 7  Even she rests. Noon is her hour, and noon ends.

Chapter 7. The Light of Compassion

 1  Compassion is the light that asks why you are giving, and waits for
    an honest answer.
 2  There are three wrong answers, and the faithful learn them early.
 3  The first wrong answer is "So that they will owe me." That is not a
    gift. That is a loan with a bow on it.
 4  The second wrong answer is "So that others will see." That is not a
    gift. That is an advertisement that happens to be warm.
 5  The third wrong answer is "So that I will feel good about myself."
    Vyrenna is gentle about this one. She says that feeling good is
    allowed, but it is the steam rising off the soup, not the soup.
 6  The right answer is the one that takes no words: because they were
    cold, and you were not.
 7  When a faithful gives for that reason, the Canon says, the coal does
    not burn their hand, and the one who receives it does not have to
    say thank you, though they usually do.

Chapter 8. The Light of Guidance

 1  The last Light is the quietest. It is knowing where your warmth
    should go.
 2  Vyrenna does not warm the whole world at once. She would smother it.
    She warms the stone the lizard is about to need.
 3  A faithful asked her, "There are so many who are cold. How do I
    choose?"
 4  She said, "Start with whoever is nearest, then whoever is forgotten,
    then whoever cannot ask."
 5  "And if I cannot reach them all?"
 6  "Then you are a person and not a sun. Warm the ones you can reach,
    and trust the Turning with the rest."
 7  This is the Light of Guidance, and it is why the faithful do not
    despair at the size of the world. The world is not theirs to warm.
    Only the stone in front of them is.

Chapter 9. The Basking Stone

 1  Every faithful keeps a basking stone. It may be a real stone. It may
    be a bench in Stormwind, a ledge in Orgrimmar, or a particular
    corner of an inn.
 2  The basking stone is the place where you stop giving for a while and
    let warmth be given to you.
 3  Vyrenna taught this to the healers first, because healers are the
    worst at it.
 4  "You cannot pour from a cold cup," she told them, which the healers
    wrote down, and then ignored, and then came back to five years
    later.
 5  To bask, sit still in the warmth. Do not check your bags. Do not
    check the group finder. Do not reply to the whisper that says "u
    there?"
 6  Bask for the length of one hymn. If there is no hymn, bask for the
    length of a long breath, three times.
 7  The Inner Scale reminds the faithful that basking is not idleness.
    It is the stone remembering the sun.

Chapter 10. The Open Palm

 1  Vyrenna's hands are always painted open, palms up. The faithful call
    this the Open Palm, and it is the first gesture every egg is taught.
 2  An open palm can give. An open palm can receive. A closed fist can
    do neither, and can only hold, and the holding hurts.
 3  Hessk the Beggar King sat at his gate with his palm open for forty
    years. People thought he was asking. He was showing them how.
 4  The Canon records that the Open Palm is also the right way to
    receive a gift: not snatching, not refusing, simply letting it be
    set down.
 5  "Many can give," said Vyrenna. "Fewer can be given to. Learn both,
    or you will only ever be half warm."
 6  When the faithful pass each other on the road, some lift an open
    palm in greeting. It means: I have nothing in my hand, and I am glad
    to see you.

Chapter 11. The Little Tithe

 1  The Little Tithe is Vyrenna's law, and it is very small on purpose.
 2  Each day, give one thing away that you did not have to give. A
    copper. A heal on a stranger. A minute of patience with a slow tank.
    An answer to a question in general chat that you could have ignored.
 3  It must be little. A great tithe makes a great show, and Vyrenna is
    not interested in shows.
 4  It must be daily. Warmth that comes once a year is a festival.
    Warmth that comes every day is a climate.
 5  It must be forgotten. The Ministry of Spare Change keeps no record
    of the Little Tithe, and anyone who keeps their own record is gently
    asked to stop.
 6  The Canon says that a guild in which every faithful pays the Little
    Tithe will never need a guild bank. The Ministry of Spare Change has
    asked that this verse be read less often.

Chapter 12. The Copper of the Famine

 1  In the hungry years of the Hidden Centuries, the Nest ran out of
    everything but copper, and copper cannot be eaten.
 2  Vyrenna came to the Keepers in a dream and told them: give each
    faithful one copper, and let them never spend it.
 3  "What good is a coin that is never spent?" the Keepers asked.
 4  "It is the coin that reminds you that you are not empty," she said.
    "A person who knows they are not empty can give the rest."
 5  So every faithful still carries a single copper that is never spent.
    It is the Copper of the Famine.
 6  When a faithful is tempted to hoard, they touch the copper and
    remember: I already have enough to not be afraid.
 7  When a faithful is tempted to despair, they touch the copper and
    remember: I am still carrying something warm.
 8  It is, the Ministry admits, the only coin in the faith that nobody
    has ever asked them for.

Chapter 13. The Lily That Asks Nothing

 1  White lilies grow at Vyrenna's feet, though nobody planted them.
 2  A gardener once asked her why the lily, of all flowers.
 3  "Because it opens all the way," said Vyrenna, "and does not close
    again to see if anyone noticed."
 4  The gardener said that roses were prettier.
 5  "Roses are lovely," said Vyrenna. "They also have thorns, in case
    someone tries to take more than they are given. I do not blame them.
    But the lily is braver."
 6  The faithful bring lilies to the sick, to the newly hatched, and to
    anyone who has had a very long week.
 7  Fire lilies are acceptable in the Firelands. The Inner Scale does
    not recommend going there to pick them.

Chapter 14. The Healer's Whisper

 1  Healers whisper Vyrenna's name before a difficult heal. The Canon
    records this plainly and adds, with some honesty, that it does not
    affect the numbers.
 2  It affects the healer.
 3  To whisper her name is to remember three things at once: that the
    warmth was never yours to begin with, that you are only passing it
    along, and that it is not your fault if the tank stood in the fire.
 4  A healer who forgets the first thing grows proud. A healer who
    forgets the second grows tired. A healer who forgets the third grows
    bitter.
 5  Vyrenna does not want her healers proud, tired or bitter. She wants
    them warm, and a little smug when the meter is good, which she
    allows.
 6  When a healer saves someone at the last possible moment, the
    faithful nearby are permitted one small cheer, and then must return
    to the fight.

Chapter 15. The Crafter's Bowl

 1  Vyrenna is also the patron of form: of things made with the hands so
    that others can use them.
 2  The first thing she ever made was a bowl. It was not beautiful. It
    was round, and it held soup, and that was enough.
 3  The faithful say that every crafted thing has a little of her in it,
    from the humblest bandage to the finest armor. The bandage may have
    more.
 4  Crafters invoke her before a difficult recipe. If the recipe fails,
    she is not to blame; the reagents were probably wrong.
 5  "Make the thing that is needed," she told the first crafters, "and
    make it so it lasts long enough to be given away twice."
 6  The Inner Scale encourages crafters to give the first thing they
    make with any new recipe to someone who cannot afford it. This is
    called the Bowl Rite, and it has made several crafters very popular
    in a short time.

Chapter 16. The Hearth and the Stranger

 1  On the coldest night of a long winter, a stranger came to an inn and
    asked for a room. The innkeeper said the inn was full.
 2  Vyrenna was sitting by the hearth in the shape of an old woman, as
    she sometimes does.
 3  She said nothing. She only moved her chair a little to the side, so
    that there was room by the fire.
 4  The innkeeper looked at the space by the fire for a long time. Then
    he sighed, and brought a blanket, and the stranger slept there.
 5  In the morning the stranger was gone, and on the hearthstone where
    he had slept lay a single warm coal that never went out.
 6  The innkeeper never learned who the stranger was. The Canon suggests
    it does not matter, and that this is the point.
 7  The faithful call it moving the chair: the smallest possible act of
    making room. Every egg is asked, in their first season, to move one
    chair for someone.

Chapter 17. The Vault Wyrm

 1  Every power has a shadow, and the Canon of the Triangle names
    Vyrenna's honestly: smothering.
 2  The Legends tell of a wyrm that loved warmth so much that it
    gathered every warm and golden thing in the world into its cave.
 3  It gathered coins, and coals, and sunlight caught in amber, and the
    fur of sleeping animals, and the songs of inns.
 4  It wrapped itself around them all, and it was warm, and it could not
    stop gathering, and it could not set any of it down.
 5  And the world outside the cave grew cold, because all its warmth was
    in one place, under one wyrm, being kept.
 6  That is the Vault Wyrm, and it is what becomes of anyone who
    worships only Vyrenna's half of the teaching.
 7  Warmth that cannot be set down is no longer warmth. It is a fever.
 8  The faithful who run raid banks are asked to read this chapter once
    a season, and to log out of the bank for a while afterwards.

Chapter 18. The Fever

 1  Vyrenna once warmed a sick child so carefully that she would not
    stop. Blanket after blanket. Fire after fire.
 2  The child grew hotter, and hotter, and began to suffer.
 3  Nhalor came quietly to the bedside, and opened a window, and took
    away one blanket.
 4  Vyrenna was angry for one breath. Then she saw the child sleep.
 5  "I wanted to give," she said.
 6  "You did," said Nhalor. "Now let it work."
 7  The Inner Scale rules against the Emberist Error on the strength of
    this chapter: the belief that the highest virtue is to give
    everything, always, to everyone, without rest. Its followers burn
    out within a season. Even the sun sets, and nobody calls the sun
    lazy.

Chapter 19. Silver and Gold

 1  The Dream Pages describe Vyrenna in noon gold. The Icon shows her in
    moon-silver. Faithful argued about this for a whole Vigil.
 2  High Priestess Xalrathiel ended the argument. Vyrenna's ember looks
    gold to those who are cold, she said, and silver to those who are
    grieving.
 3  The cold need heat, and so they see the gold. The grieving need
    gentleness, and so they see the moon.
 4  The painter of the Icon, it was later learned, had been grieving
    that week.
 5  So the faithful do not argue about her colors. They ask instead:
    what color is she to you today? And the answer tells them what they
    need.
 6  Some faithful report seeing her violet. Xalrathiel says this means
    you have been looking at the Coil too long and should go outside.

Chapter 20. The Salamander's Lesson

 1  The Basking Salamanders of the Warm Ridge are her sacred animal, and
    they are very bad at being worshipped. They mostly sleep.
 2  The Canon says they learned stillness from Xal'Reth and warmth from
    Vyrenna, and so they are a living lesson in two corners of the
    Triangle at once.
 3  A salamander lies on a warm stone. It does not hoard the stone. When
    another salamander arrives, it shifts over.
 4  It does not give the stone away either. It simply makes room, and
    both of them are warm.
 5  "Be like the salamander," Vyrenna told the eggs. "Take only the
    warmth you need, and shift over when someone else arrives."
 6  "What if a bigger salamander takes the whole stone?" asked one egg.
 7  "Then find another stone," said Vyrenna, "and leave the bigger
    salamander to the Accusers."

Chapter 21. The Feast of Scales

 1  The Feast of Scales belongs to Vyrenna, and it falls at Midsummer,
    on the warmest day of the Cult's year.
 2  On that day the faithful bask long, eat together, and give one warm
    gift: a meal, a cloak, an invitation, a summons on a cold night.
 3  The gift must be given to someone outside your usual circle. Giving
    to your best friend is lovely, but it is not the Feast.
 4  At noon the whole Cult falls silent for the length of one hymn and
    faces the sun. Players in raids are excused. Players in queue are
    not.
 5  The feast table is set with an empty seat for the stranger who has
    not arrived yet. If no stranger arrives, the empty seat is warmed by
    the sun and that is considered sufficient.
 6  The Feast ends when the sun begins to set, and the faithful hand the
    day to Nhalor with gratitude, because a feast that never ends is
    just a very long lunch.

Chapter 22. The Proverbs of the Warm Palm

 1  Open the hand, and the coal will not burn you. Close the hand, and
    even gold will.
 2  The light that is given away does not run out. That is the only
    miracle she has ever claimed.
 3  A warm word costs nothing and is still the most expensive thing some
    people have ever been given.
 4  Do not wait to be warm before you warm someone. The ember was cold
    once too.
 5  A full belly forgives many insults. Feed your enemy before you argue
    with them.
 6  Generosity with a ledger is just commerce in a nicer robe.
 7  If you give and are not thanked, the gift was still received. Ask
    Nhalor.
 8  The healer who never rests becomes the patient. Sit on your stone.
 9  Make room by the fire. You will not lose any of the heat.

Chapter 23. The Hymn of Noon

 1  This hymn is sung at noon on feast days, facing the sun, with the
    palms open and up.
 2  Warm the stone and warm the hand, warm the stranger where they
    stand.
 3  Give the bread and give the flame, give, and do not ask a name.
 4  Light that leaves us is not lost; light that's hoarded counts the
    cost.
 5  Silver, gold, whatever hue, she is warm enough for you.
 6  Noon will pass and dusk will fall; give it now, and give it all.
 7  Ember-Scale, we lift our palms. Here is warmth. Here are our arms.
 8  (The last line is sometimes sung "Here is warmth, and here are
    alms," by members of the Ministry of Spare Change. They have been
    asked not to.)

Chapter 24. Through Endless Light

 1  Beneath her on the Icon are written the words: Through endless
    light.
 2  The faithful once thought this meant that Vyrenna's light never
    ends. That is almost right.
 3  It means that light given away passes through endless hands. You
    warm one person. They warm another. Somewhere far down the line,
    someone you will never meet is a little less cold because of what
    you did this morning.
 4  That is the endless light. It is not one great fire. It is a great
    many small ones, handed along.
 5  When Vyrenna has finished giving, she does not hold on to the
    gratitude. She passes that along too, to Nhalor, who sets it down
    gently in the Void, where there is room.
 6  And Xal'Reth, beneath them both, feels the warmth go by, and holds
    the world a little more easily.
 7  Here ends the Radiant Scroll. Warmth received. Warmth given.

BOOK II: THE BOOK OF XAL'RETH
The Low Scroll

He who holds. Twenty-four chapters of the Coil Beneath, read aloud at
midnight, sitting as low as you can.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Chapter 1. The Egg That Nobody Laid

 1  In the beginning there was an Egg, drifting in the cold Void, and
    nobody had laid it.
 2  The Listeners have asked for ten thousand years who laid the Egg,
    and the Coil has never answered, and the faithful have come to think
    the silence is the answer.
 3  Some things simply arrive. You did not lay them either. You only get
    to decide what to do once they hatch.
 4  The Egg waited a while, and then longer than that. The Canon counts
    this as the first patience in the world, and notes that it was not
    yet anyone's patience. It was only waiting.
 5  Waiting becomes patience when someone chooses it. That would come
    later.
 6  When the Egg hatched, it did not break into one piece but two: the
    shell, which became the Scale, and the hollow, which became the
    Void.
 7  And from the place where they met, something crawled out. It was
    hungry.

Chapter 2. The Restless Serpent

 1  What came out of the Egg was the Restless Serpent, and the age it
    ruled is called the Age of the Open Jaw.
 2  It swam the dark devouring drifting stones, starlight and the
    cinders of unformed worlds.
 3  Every world it swallowed made it larger, and every inch it grew made
    it hungrier.
 4  It was not cruel. The Canon is careful about this. It simply wanted,
    and wanting was all it knew.
 5  The faithful are asked to remember this chapter whenever they are
    tempted to think themselves above the Grippers and the hoarders. The
    god of patience began as the very thing he now teaches us to let go
    of.
 6  He does not ask the faithful for a stillness he was born with. He
    asks for a stillness he had to learn.
 7  That is why he is kind to those who are still learning it.

Chapter 3. The Four Sights

 1  Then he came upon a young world, and opened his jaws to swallow it,
    and four small things stopped him.
 2  The first sight was a shed skin drifting at the edge of the world,
    shaped exactly like him, and completely empty.
 3  The second sight was a broken eggshell on a shore, left behind by a
    creature that had hatched and gone.
 4  The third sight was a dry riverbed, where water once ran.
 5  The fourth sight was a newborn lizard asleep on a sun-warmed stone,
    wanting nothing, perfectly at peace.
 6  He looked at the empty skin and understood that what he had been
    could be left behind. He looked at the shell and understood that
    every beginning is also an ending. He looked at the riverbed and
    understood that nothing that flows can be kept.
 7  And he looked at the lizard for a very long time, because he did not
    understand it at all, and he wanted to.
 8  He closed his jaws. It was the first thing in his life he had not
    eaten.

Chapter 4. The Night of Stillness

 1  He lowered himself beneath the young world and lay still.
 2  The Night of Stillness lasted one night, and also ten thousand
    years. The Canon insists on both numbers and refuses to reconcile
    them.
 3  For the first hundred years, he was only hungry and still, which is
    not the same as peaceful.
 4  For the next thousand years, he was still and listening, and began
    to hear the young world above him: the first rains, the first
    footsteps, the first lizards finding their stones.
 5  For the rest of the night, he was simply still, and the hunger,
    without anything to eat, slowly became something else.
 6  The Scale lay against one side of him, and the Void lay against the
    other, and during all those years they listened to him too, and
    learned to be someone. That is how Vyrenna and Nhalor came to be. He
    did not make them. He kept still long enough that they could make
    themselves.
 7  The faithful say this is the only way anyone is ever truly made.

Chapter 5. Ssyrr's Three Temptations

 1  In the deepest part of the night, his hunger took a shape and came
    to tempt him. Its name was Ssyrr.
 2  Ssyrr came first as a feast: a thousand worlds, ripe and glowing,
    all his for the opening of a jaw. He did not open his jaw.
 3  Ssyrr came second as a throne: the worship of every creature on the
    young world, bowing to the greatest power in the Void. He did not
    lift his head.
 4  Ssyrr came third as a fear, and this was the hardest. It whispered
    that if he stopped gripping, he would dissolve into nothing. That
    stillness was only slow death. That he must hold on to something,
    anything, or be lost.
 5  He nearly listened. His great claw began to close.
 6  Then he pressed his belly against the warm underside of the world,
    and felt it hold him, and answered: "I am not lost. I am held. And
    if I am held, I can hold."
 7  Ssyrr had no reply to that, and has never had one since. It still
    comes, to everyone, in all three shapes. The faithful are taught the
    answer before they are taught anything else.

Chapter 6. The Unclasped Claw

 1  Even after his answer, the claw did not open at once. It had closed
    around a drifting fragment of a broken world and held on, tighter
    and tighter, until the fragment began to crack.
 2  Nhalor came to the claw and did not pry it open. He sat beside it in
    the dark, very quietly, for a hundred years.
 3  At the end of the hundred years the claw was tired, and it opened on
    its own, and the fragment drifted free, whole again.
 4  "Why did you not take it from me?" the serpent asked.
 5  "Because then you would have lost it," said Nhalor. "This way, you
    let it go. That is a different thing."
 6  The great image of Xal'Reth the Eternal shows one claw open, holding
    nothing, and at peace.
 7  When a faithful cannot let go of something, the others do not pry.
    They sit beside them, quietly, for as long as it takes. A hundred
    years is the upper limit. Most things take an evening.

Chapter 7. The Open Door

 1  When the serpent opened his single great Eye at the end of the
    night, he understood that hunger comes from gripping, and gripping
    comes from believing you are separate from everything you reach for.
 2  And before him stood the Open Door: perfect peace, freedom from all
    wanting, the place where everything that ends goes through.
 3  It was his for the taking. He had earned it. Nobody in the Void
    would have blamed him.
 4  He looked through the Door, and it was beautiful, and there was
    nothing to hold on the other side.
 5  Then he looked down at the young world, wobbling and cracking, and
    at the tiny creatures beginning to stir on it, creatures who would
    grow and want and grip and suffer exactly as he had.
 6  He did not take the Door.
 7  The faithful say the most important word in the Canon is the word
    "stay," and that he never actually said it. He just did not leave.
    Most staying is like that.

Chapter 8. The First Vow

 1  He wrapped the young world once, and spoke the First Vow.
 2  "I will hold this world until every creature on it has learned to
    let go."
 3  It is the longest promise ever made, and it has a condition at the
    end of it, and the condition is about us.
 4  He did not say "until the end of time," which would have been
    easier. He did not say "forever," which would have been grander. He
    said until every creature has learned.
 5  The faithful take this personally. Every grudge they keep, every
    coin they clutch, every argument they refuse to drop is, in a small
    way, keeping him down there longer.
 6  This is not said to make anyone feel guilty. Xal'Reth does not mind
    waiting. It is said so that the faithful know their letting go is
    not small. It is someone's freedom, and not only theirs.
 7  He became Xal'Reth in that moment, which in the old tongue of the
    Listeners means the one who is still here, and also the one who is
    still hungry, because he never ate the world. The name means both.
    He does too.

Chapter 9. The Three Wrappings

 1  He wrapped the world three times, and each wrapping has a meaning.
 2  The first wrapping holds the world together, so that it does not
    crack. This is the Outer Turn, the coil of the world and of how we
    treat each other.
 3  The second wrapping holds the world's memory, so that nothing that
    happened is lost, even when it is let go. This is the Middle Turn,
    the coil of memory and dreams.
 4  The third wrapping holds the world still, at its very heart, so that
    there is always one quiet place. This is the Inner Turn, where
    holding, giving and releasing become one motion.
 5  The Canon says that every creature lives inside all three wrappings
    at once, whether they know it or not.
 6  When a faithful feels the world shaking, they are asked to name
    which wrapping they are worried about. It is usually the first. It
    is usually fine.
 7  The three wrappings are also why the faithful bow three times, and
    why Khaedara counts.

Chapter 10. The Low Place

 1  He is called the Coil Beneath because the freest being in the Void
    chose the lowest place, and has stayed there ever since.
 2  Water seeks the low place, and so the faithful honor water. Valleys
    are low, and so the faithful honor valleys. Foundations are low, and
    so the faithful honor foundations, and the people who pour them, and
    the guild members who do the boring logistics.
 3  At the feast, the humble seat at the bottom of the table is called
    his seat, and the Inner Scale fights quietly over who gets to sit in
    it.
 4  To be low is not to be small. A mountain rests on what is beneath
    it. So does a raid.
 5  "I am beneath you," he said, "so that you do not have to be beneath
    anything."
 6  The faithful who are mocked, overlooked or left out of the group are
    told: you are in good company. The greatest power in the Void is
    down there with you, and he is not embarrassed about it.

Chapter 11. The Unblinking Eye

 1  He has one great Eye, and it does not blink. Faithful children are
    sometimes frightened by this. They should not be.
 2  The Eye does not blink because it is always watching to see whether
    you have let go yet. Kindly.
 3  It does not watch to judge. Judgment belongs to the Accusers, and
    even they only accuse people of very minor crimes.
 4  It watches the way a parent watches a child learning to walk: ready,
    patient, and a little proud even of the falls.
 5  The Eye sits between the Scale and the Void, and the Canon says this
    is what keeps them safe. Vyrenna alone would smother. Nhalor alone
    would dissolve. Seen together by the Eye, they become warmth that
    knows when to stop and release that knows when to stay.
 6  The faithful greet one another with the slow double blink, which is
    the closest anyone can come to his unblinking gaze. It means: I see
    you, and I am in no hurry.

Chapter 12. The Four Turnings

 1  On the Icon of the Three he is painted above four words:
    Transformation, Power, Evolution, Dominion. These are called the
    Four Turnings.
 2  The Inner Scale annotated two of them almost immediately, because
    Warm-Blooded readers kept taking them the wrong way.
 3  The Four Turnings are not four things he does to the world. They are
    four things he did to himself, so that he could hold the world
    without crushing it.
 4  He transformed from the Restless Serpent into the Coil. He found a
    power that does not grab. He evolved, slowly, through the long
    night. And he took dominion over the only thing he ever truly rules:
    his own claw.
 5  The next four chapters read the Turnings one at a time. Read them
    slowly. He did.

Chapter 13. Transformation

 1  The first Turning is Transformation, and it is the story of the shed
    skin.
 2  The serpent saw his old skin drifting at the edge of the world,
    empty, and understood that he did not have to stay the shape he had
    been.
 3  This is the good news of the Coil, the Canon says, and it is for
    everyone. You are not the hunger you were born with. You can shed
    it. It will drift away, still shaped like you, and you will be
    surprised how light you feel.
 4  Transformation is not becoming someone else. It is becoming who you
    were underneath the old skin.
 5  A faithful who changes their spec, their main, their guild, their
    mind or their ways is not betraying who they were. They are molting.
 6  The Rite of Shedding is performed in honor of this Turning, and the
    words are always the same: send it after the First Skin.

Chapter 14. Power

 1  The second Turning is Power, and the Inner Scale has annotated it in
    the margin of every copy: "not that kind."
 2  The power of Xal'Reth is not the power to take. He had that, in the
    Age of the Open Jaw, and it only made him hungrier.
 3  His power is the power to hold without gripping. To bear weight
    without being crushed by it. To carry the world and still have a
    claw free.
 4  "The strong one is not the one who can lift the most," the Canon
    says. "It is the one who can set it down."
 5  The faithful are taught to distrust any power that has to keep
    proving itself. A dam does not shout. It holds.
 6  Tanks are asked to meditate on this Turning before a long pull. It
    does not increase their armor. It increases how calm they sound on
    voice chat, which the Canon considers the greater gift.

Chapter 15. Evolution

 1  The third Turning is Evolution, and it is the slowest of the four.
 2  Nothing about the Coil happened quickly. The Egg waited. The night
    lasted ten thousand years. The claw took a hundred to open.
 3  Evolution is the faith that slow change is still change. That a
    little less gripping today, and a little less tomorrow, adds up to a
    serpent who can hold the world.
 4  The Restless Serpent did not become the Coil in a single night. He
    became it one stillness at a time, and the faithful are expected to
    do the same.
 5  A faithful who complains that they have been practising letting go
    for a whole month and still get angry in the auction house is gently
    reminded that he took ten thousand years, and he was a god.
 6  "Every coil," says the Canon, "is made of many small turns."

Chapter 16. Dominion

 1  The fourth Turning is Dominion, and the Inner Scale has annotated it
    in large letters: "over his own claw."
 2  Xal'Reth rules nothing. He holds everything and commands nothing,
    and this confuses those who come looking for a god of empire.
 3  The only thing he has ever ruled is himself: his hunger, his grip,
    his fear. That is the dominion painted on the Icon.
 4  "He who rules the world rules nothing," the Canon says. "He who
    rules his own claw can hold the world."
 5  Faithful who seek dominion over others are directed to this chapter,
    and then to Khaedara, who will accuse them of a Minor Crime to keep
    things light.
 6  The faithful are encouraged to seek dominion over exactly one thing
    each day: the urge to type the reply they will regret.

Chapter 17. The Shadow of Gripping

 1  Every power has a shadow, and the Eternal's shadow is the one his
    name warns about: gripping.
 2  Holding without giving becomes a hoard. Holding without release
    becomes a fist.
 3  During the Night of Stillness, Ssyrr nearly taught him this, and his
    claw nearly closed for good.
 4  A Xal'Reth with no Vyrenna and no Nhalor would not be a god. He
    would be a very large, very cold, very tight snake.
 5  This is why he does not hold the world alone. Vyrenna warms what he
    holds, so that holding does not become a cage. Nhalor releases what
    he has held long enough, so that holding does not become a prison.
 6  The faithful practise holding lightly, which the greeting teaches:
    Hold light. It does not mean hold weakly. It means hold the way you
    would hold a sleeping lizard: firmly enough that it will not fall,
    gently enough that it does not wake.

Chapter 18. The Grippers

 1  The Bestiary of the Void records the Grippers, who feed on
    clutching.
 2  They are small, and grey, and cling to whatever a faithful is
    holding too tightly: a grudge, a title, a full bag of things they
    will never use, a loot roll they lost three weeks ago.
 3  A Gripper cannot be fought. Fighting it is a kind of clutching, and
    it only grows.
 4  A Gripper can only be starved. Loosen the hand, and it falls away on
    its own, confused, and goes looking for someone else.
 5  The Wardens of the Void keep a list of signs that a Gripper has
    attached itself: rereading an old argument, checking the auction
    house every four minutes, refusing to vendor anything grey because
    it might be useful someday.
 6  Xal'Reth, the Canon says, was once the greatest Gripper of all. That
    is how he knows how to get rid of them.

Chapter 19. On Waiting

 1  Xal'Reth is the patron of everyone who waits: in queues, on long
    flights, at the bench at the end of a dungeon, in the lobby for the
    one person who is still "just grabbing a drink."
 2  Waiting is his oldest practice. Before he was patient, he was only
    waiting, and the difference was a choice.
 3  The faithful are taught the Three Waits. To wait in anger is to be
    gripped. To wait in boredom is to be absent. To wait in stillness is
    to be with him.
 4  "I have waited ten thousand years," he says in the Dream Pages, "and
    I did not waste a single one."
 5  When the queue pops, the faithful are permitted to stop meditating
    immediately. He understands.
 6  A faithful who has waited well is said to have kept a small Vigil,
    and the Inner Scale counts these, unofficially, with great
    affection.

Chapter 20. The Tank's Breath

 1  The tanks of the Cult honor Xal'Reth before every pull by standing
    still for one breath.
 2  It is called the Tank's Breath, and it is the shortest rite in the
    faith.
 3  In that breath the tank remembers that they are about to take the
    weight so that others do not have to. That they will hold the line.
    That they do not need to move unless moving is necessary.
 4  The Canon says that the best tank is like the Coil: everyone stands
    on them, and nobody has to think about them, and that is the highest
    compliment.
 5  If the tank dies, the faithful do not blame them. Even the Coil
    cracked once, a little, in the Age of Tightening.
 6  If the healer dies, the faithful also do not blame the tank,
    although they are permitted to look at the tank for slightly longer
    than usual.

Chapter 21. No Wings

 1  Xal'Reth has no wings. This chapter exists because this needs
    saying, regularly.
 2  The Featherist Heresy teaches that he has wings, great dark ones,
    folded somewhere out of sight, and that he will one day spread them
    and fly up out of the world.
 3  He will not. He chose to stay. Flight is the opposite of what he is.
 4  The Icon-Painters were asked, firmly, before they began. The flowing
    shapes behind him on the Icon are his cloak, his coils and the
    world's weather.
 5  The Featherists were briefly thrilled by Nhalor's hollow wings,
    which the Canon of the Triangle does acknowledge. They are not the
    same thing. They are not that kind of wings. Go home.
 6  The Inner Scale asks that this chapter be read aloud whenever a new
    egg asks, in all innocence, "but what if he does have wings though?"
    Everyone in the room is then permitted one deep sigh.

Chapter 22. The Vigil

 1  The Vigil is his rite: sitting still, in one place, longer than is
    comfortable, for no reward.
 2  It is performed at midnight, with the hands set low and cupped,
    holding nothing.
 3  Nothing happens during the Vigil. That is what happens during the
    Vigil.
 4  A faithful once complained to the High Priestess that the Vigil was
    boring. "Yes," said Xalrathiel. "So was the Night of Stillness, at
    first."
 5  The faithful are allowed to think during the Vigil, but not to grip
    their thoughts. Let them come, let them sit for a while, let them
    go, the way travellers pass through an inn.
 6  At the end of the Vigil the faithful rise, slowly, and say nothing,
    and go to bed. In the morning they usually find they are holding
    something a little more lightly, and cannot say what.

Chapter 23. The Hymn of Midnight

 1  This hymn is sung at midnight, low, with the hands cupped beneath
    the heart.
 2  Down below the lowest stone, there is one who's not alone.
 3  Down below the roots and rain, one who held and holds again.
 4  Still as water, low as ground, not a sound and not a sound.
 5  Hunger once, and stillness now, open claw and level brow.
 6  Hold the world, but hold it light; hold us gently through the night.
 7  Coil beneath and Eye awake, take no more than you can take.
 8  (The final line confused the Choir of the Hiss for some years. The
    High Priestess explained that it is not a request to Xal'Reth. It is
    a reminder to the singer.)

Chapter 24. Beyond All Things

 1  Beneath him on the Icon are written the words: Beyond all things.
 2  The Warm-Blooded read this as "above all things," and imagine a god
    on a high throne. The faithful smile, and do not correct them right
    away.
 3  He is beneath all things, which, from the Coil's point of view, is
    the same place. To be under everything is to be beyond it. Nothing
    can fall below him. Everything rests on him.
 4  He is beyond hunger, because he chose not to eat. Beyond the Door,
    because he chose not to go through. Beyond all things, because he
    chose to hold them.
 5  And when the last creature on the young world has finally learned to
    let go, the Unwrapping will come, gently, the way a hand opens after
    a long sleep. And he will go through the Door at last, and all of us
    with him, together.
 6  Until then he is still here. That is what his name means.
 7  Here ends the Low Scroll. Hold light. And let go well.

BOOK III: THE BOOK OF NHALOR
The Veiled Scroll

He who releases. Twenty-four chapters of the Hollow Wing, read aloud at
dusk, quietly, with the palms facing down.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Chapter 1. The Hollow Wakes

 1  On the other side of the Coil from the shell lay the hollow: all
    that is empty, open, cold and still. It was the Void.
 2  For ten thousand years it lay against the serpent's side and
    listened to him keep the Night of Stillness.
 3  It had no form, so it could not take a face the way the Scale did.
    It had no warmth, so it could not give.
 4  What it learned instead was how to make room. Every time the serpent
    let a little hunger go, the hunger fell into the Void, and the Void
    held the space open for it, and asked nothing.
 5  It woke fully on the day Vyrenna poured her first warmth into it and
    wept, thinking the gift refused.
 6  "It was received," said the Void, and in saying so became Nhalor,
    who receives without keeping.
 7  The Listeners say he is the only one of the three who was born by
    saying thank you.

Chapter 2. The Untying

 1  The Warm-Blooded who hear of a god of endings imagine a reaper with
    a blade. The faithful know better.
 2  Nhalor does not end things. He lets things end.
 3  The Canon says there is a difference between cutting a rope and
    untying it, and Nhalor has never cut anything.
 4  To cut is quick, and leaves a frayed end on both sides. To untie is
    slow, and leaves the rope whole, so it can be used again.
 5  When a friendship ends, a season closes, a guild disbands or a main
    is retired, the faithful ask Nhalor to help them untie it rather
    than cut it.
 6  They do this by naming what the thing gave them, out loud, before
    they set it down.
 7  "I do not take," Nhalor says. "I only hold the door while you put it
    down."

Chapter 3. The Keeper of Shed Skins

 1  Every shed skin in the world passes through Nhalor's hands on its
    way into the Void.
 2  Old habits. Old names. Old grudges. The armor you wore before you
    found the set you actually liked.
 3  He handles each one with care, because he knows it once mattered.
    Nothing that was ever worn by someone is treated as rubbish.
 4  The Hall of Shed Skins, which the Legends describe as an endless
    gallery in the deep Void, is said to be his. Every skin hangs there,
    empty, at peace, shaped exactly like the one who left it.
 5  The faithful are not frightened of the Hall. It is a place of great
    quiet, and many report that the skins there look relieved.
 6  When a faithful sheds something hard, they are told: it will be kept
    somewhere gentle. You do not have to carry it to keep it from being
    lost.

Chapter 4. The Four Silences

 1  On the Icon of the Three he is painted above four words: Secrets,
    Destiny, Silence, Knowledge. These are called the Four Silences.
 2  They are the four things a faithful can only receive by being quiet.
 3  Vyrenna's Lights are given. Xal'Reth's Turnings are practised.
    Nhalor's Silences are listened for.
 4  The Inner Scale asks that the next four chapters be read more slowly
    than the others, and with fewer people talking over the reader.
 5  The Choir of the Hiss has asked whether hissing counts as talking.
    It does.

Chapter 5. Secrets

 1  The first Silence is Secrets, and it is not what the Warm-Blooded
    think.
 2  Nhalor does not keep secrets in order to have power over anyone. He
    keeps the things that were told to him in the dark, when someone
    needed to put them down.
 3  A secret kept by Nhalor is a burden someone no longer has to carry
    alone. That is all.
 4  The faithful who hear confessions, the Wardens who sit with the
    troubled, and the friends who are told the thing nobody else knows
    are all said to be doing Nhalor's work.
 5  They are bound by his rule: what is set down in the dark is not
    picked up in the light. Not for gossip, not for leverage, and not
    for a funny story in guild chat.
 6  Khaedara the Accuser has noted that breaking this rule is one of the
    very few crimes she does not consider minor.

Chapter 6. Destiny

 1  The second Silence is Destiny, and the faithful have argued about it
    more than any of the others.
 2  Some say Nhalor knows how everything ends, because every ending
    passes through him.
 3  The Canon says something gentler. Nhalor knows that everything ends.
    He does not know how, and he does not want to. He simply knows that
    the door will be there, and that he will be holding it.
 4  Destiny, in the faith of the Coil, is not a road already drawn. It
    is the certainty that whatever road you take, there will be rest at
    the end of it.
 5  A faithful who is afraid of the future is told: you do not know what
    will happen, and neither does he. But you know who will be waiting.
 6  The Inner Scale does not permit fortune-telling in Nhalor's name.
    Fortune-telling in Vyrenna's name is permitted at the Feast of
    Scales, for fun, and is always good news.

Chapter 7. Silence

 1  The third Silence is Silence itself, and there is very little to say
    about it, which is the point.
 2  Nhalor speaks rarely. When he speaks, it is usually one sentence,
    and it is usually the right one.
 3  The faithful are taught that silence is not empty. It is a room with
    the door held open, waiting for whatever needs to come in.
 4  In an argument, the faithful are encouraged to keep Nhalor's silence
    for one breath before answering. Most arguments are shorter
    afterwards.
 5  At the death of a faithful, the guild keeps Nhalor's Minute: one
    full minute of silence in guild chat. Nobody posts. Nobody links an
    item. Somebody always breaks it by accident, and everyone forgives
    them, and that is also part of the rite.
 6  The rest of this chapter is left blank on purpose.

Chapter 8. Knowledge

 1  The fourth Silence is Knowledge, and it is the knowledge that only
    comes after letting go.
 2  You cannot see a thing clearly while you are gripping it. Your hand
    is in the way.
 3  When a faithful finally sets something down, a grudge, a fear, an
    old plan that was never going to work, they often see it properly
    for the first time. What it was. Why they held it. What it cost.
 4  Nhalor is the keeper of that knowledge. He gives it gently, after
    the release, never before.
 5  "I will tell you what it meant," he says in the Dream Pages, "once
    you are no longer afraid of the answer."
 6  The faithful call this knowing by the empty hand, and they consider
    it the deepest wisdom in the Triangle, deeper even than the Light of
    Wisdom, which Vyrenna, to her credit, agrees with.

Chapter 9. The Hollow Wings

 1  Nhalor has wings. The Canon of the Triangle says so plainly, and the
    Featherists have never recovered from the excitement.
 2  They are hollow wings: frames of pale silver bone with nothing
    stretched between them.
 3  They cannot fly. They are not for flying. They are for letting the
    wind pass through.
 4  On the Icon he is painted with no wings showing at all, because he
    keeps them folded inside his cloak. They only show when he opens the
    cloak to take something you have finally set down.
 5  The painter of the Icon was not setting anything down that week.
    Hence, no wings in the painting.
 6  The Featherists ask whether this proves that Xal'Reth might have
    wings too. It does not. They are not that kind of wings. Go home.
 7  The faithful sometimes see the hollow wings in dreams, after a great
    release. They are said to look like a door made of moonlight,
    standing open.

Chapter 10. The Eclipse

 1  On the Icon, Nhalor stands before an eclipse.
 2  The faithful say the eclipse is light that has stepped aside for a
    while, so you can see the stars.
 3  Vyrenna's light is good. But even good light can hide things. There
    are stars in the sky at noon; you simply cannot see them.
 4  Nhalor is the moment the bright thing steps aside, and you finally
    notice everything else that was there all along: the friend who was
    quietly helping, the small joy you had stopped seeing, the stars.
 5  An eclipse is never permanent. The light comes back. Nhalor does not
    take the sun. He borrows it, briefly, to show you something.
 6  When a faithful loses something bright and good, they are told to
    look up. Not because the loss is small, but because there may be
    stars.

Chapter 11. The Warden of the Door

 1  When a faithful returns to the Coil, Nhalor walks them to the Open
    Door.
 2  He does not push them through. He does not hurry them. He waits with
    them there, for as long as they need, and holds the door.
 3  The Canon says that nobody goes through the Door alone, and that
    nobody goes through it before they are ready, even if it seems that
    way to those left behind.
 4  This is why the faithful do not fear endings as much as the Warm-
    Blooded do. They know who is waiting at the door, and they know he
    is patient, and they know he once sat beside a clenched claw for a
    hundred years.
 5  Those who grieve are told: they were walked there gently. They were
    not afraid by the end. Nhalor saw to that.
 6  And when the Unwrapping comes, the Door will open for everyone at
    once, and Nhalor will hold it for all of us, and Xal'Reth will go
    through last.

Chapter 12. Nhalor's Minute

 1  When a faithful returns to the Coil, the guild stands in silence for
    one minute. It is called Nhalor's Minute.
 2  It is also kept when a guild disbands, when a long-time member
    leaves for good, and when a server closes.
 3  During the minute, nobody speaks, nobody links an item and nobody
    pulls. Players in combat are excused. Players in the auction house
    are not.
 4  At the end of the minute, someone says the name of the one who has
    gone, and everyone else answers: "Set down, not lost."
 5  The faithful are taught that grief is not a failure to let go. Grief
    is how letting go feels from the inside. Nhalor does not hurry it.
 6  "Weep," he says in the Dream Pages. "The Void has room for all of
    it."

Chapter 13. Nhalor's Mark

 1  In the Ledger kept by the Accusers, every Minor Crime is written
    beside the name of the one who committed it.
 2  When the crime is forgiven, a small coil is drawn beside it. Among
    the Accusers this is called Nhalor's mark.
 3  The crime is not erased. The Ledger remembers everything; that is
    Xal'Reth's second wrapping. But once Nhalor's mark is beside it, it
    is no longer held against anyone.
 4  Khaedara the Accuser is said to draw more of Nhalor's marks than any
    Accuser in history. She accuses freely and forgives faster.
 5  The faithful are encouraged to draw Nhalor's mark beside their own
    old mistakes too, in whatever private ledger they keep.
 6  When a grudge is forgiven, the faithful blow gently across their
    palm, as if blowing away dust. That is the mark, drawn in air.

Chapter 14. The Grey Item

 1  There is a chapter in the Veiled Scroll about grey items, and the
    Inner Scale has been asked many times to remove it, and has refused.
 2  Every adventurer carries grey items: worn boots, broken fangs, a
    cracked bowl from a dungeon long ago. They are worth almost nothing.
    The adventurer keeps them anyway.
 3  "It might be useful," they say. It will not be. They know it will
    not be.
 4  Nhalor's teaching is simple. Vendor the grey item. Feel how light
    the bag becomes. Notice that nothing bad happened.
 5  The faithful practise this as a small rite of release. If you cannot
    let go of a broken fang, you will not be ready to let go of a broken
    heart.
 6  The vendor, the Canon adds, is doing holy work, and should be
    thanked.

Chapter 15. The Pale Moth

 1  The pale moth is Nhalor's sacred animal.
 2  It spends its youth wrapped tight in a cocoon, holding on. Then one
    night it breaks free and leaves the cocoon behind without a backward
    glance.
 3  It does not mourn the cocoon. It does not carry it around. It does
    not explain to the other moths how important the cocoon was.
 4  It simply flies toward the nearest light, which is usually a
    lantern, which the faithful admit is not always wise. Even sacred
    animals have flaws.
 5  "Be like the moth," Nhalor taught. "Leave the shell. Go toward the
    light. Watch out for the lantern."
 6  Moths that land on a faithful during dusk prayers are considered a
    blessing and must not be swatted, even by mages.

Chapter 16. Ulasha's Empty Sack

 1  Ulasha the Wanderer walked across the world with a sack on her back,
    and the sack was always empty.
 2  People asked her why she carried an empty sack.
 3  "So that I always have room," she said, "for whatever someone needs
    to give away."
 4  She would take a grief, a fear or a bad memory from a stranger, put
    it in the sack, carry it for a few miles, and then set it down by
    the road for Nhalor to collect.
 5  The sack was never heavy, because she never kept anything in it for
    long.
 6  The empty sack of Ulasha is Nhalor's sacred object, and every Warden
    of the Void is given a small cloth bag on the day they are named,
    with nothing in it. They are expected to keep it that way.

Chapter 17. The Cold Stone

 1  In the Age of the Warm-Blooded there was a village that had
    forgotten every god, and it was cold, and its people were cold to
    each other.
 2  Vyrenna wanted to warm it, but whenever she warmed a stone, the
    villagers fought over who would sit on it.
 3  So Vyrenna asked Nhalor for help.
 4  That night Nhalor walked through the village and took from each
    villager one small thing they did not need: a grudge here, a fear
    there, a hoarded coin, an old insult.
 5  In the morning the villagers woke strangely light. Vyrenna warmed a
    single stone in the square, and this time, nobody fought over it.
    Someone sat down. Then someone sat beside them. Then a child climbed
    into a lap.
 6  Warmth cannot enter a full hand. Release cannot help an empty heart.
    The Coil holds the space where they meet.
 7  The faithful say Nhalor's part in this story is often forgotten,
    because nobody notices the absence of a grudge. He prefers it that
    way.

Chapter 18. The Shedwraiths

 1  Every power has a shadow, and the Canon of the Triangle names
    Nhalor's honestly: drifting.
 2  Release without holding becomes abandonment. Rest without return
    becomes absence.
 3  The Legends tell of the Shedwraiths, who worshipped only Nhalor's
    half of the teaching. They let go of everything: their duties, their
    friends, their names, and finally themselves.
 4  Now they wander the Void, hollow and unanchored, looking a little
    like shed skins that have forgotten they were once worn.
 5  They are not evil. They are not even unhappy, exactly. They are
    simply not anywhere.
 6  A Nhalor who never held would let the whole world slip away and call
    it peace. This is why he stands beside Vyrenna and not alone, and
    why he rests on Xal'Reth, who holds.

Chapter 19. The Hollow Error

 1  The Inner Scale rules against the Hollow Error: the belief that the
    highest virtue is to let go of everything, including duty and
    friendship.
 2  Its followers drift away from the guild and are rarely heard from
    again. Sometimes they leave a message saying they have "transcended
    raid night." Nobody is fooled.
 3  The Canon is gentle with them. Letting go is a good teaching that
    has been held too tightly, which is a very funny thing to do with a
    teaching about letting go.
 4  The Keepers of the Egg sit with them, warmly, until they come back.
    Vyrenna is invoked. Xal'Reth is invoked. Somebody usually brings
    soup.
 5  "I release what I have held long enough," Nhalor says. "I do not
    release what I was asked to carry for a friend."
 6  A faithful who wants to step back from the guild is welcome to, with
    Nhalor's blessing. They are simply asked to say goodbye, so that it
    is an untying and not a cut.

Chapter 20. Shedfall

 1  Shedfall is the autumn holy season, and it belongs to Nhalor.
 2  In Shedfall every faithful sheds one thing before witnesses: a
    habit, a grudge, an old title, a thing in the bank nobody has
    touched in a year.
 3  The shed thing is named aloud. The witnesses answer: "Send it after
    the First Skin."
 4  The guild bank is cleared during Shedfall of everything untouched
    for a full year. The Ministry of Spare Change weeps quietly through
    this, and is comforted.
 5  The leaves fall in Shedfall, and the faithful see in them Nhalor's
    whole teaching: the tree does not fight to keep them, and it is not
    smaller in spring.
 6  Shedfall ends on the first truly cold night, when the faithful
    gather, light no fire, and sit together in the dark for a while,
    lighter than they were.

Chapter 21. The Rest Between

 1  Nhalor is also the god of rest: of sleep, of the pause between
    efforts, of the bench at the end of the dungeon.
 2  The faithful are taught that rest is not the opposite of devotion.
    It is one of its corners.
 3  A day of holding and giving with no release in it is a day that will
    break you, slowly, the way a bowstring breaks if it is never
    unstrung.
 4  Before sleep, the faithful say: "Nhalor, keep the door." It means: I
    am setting the day down. Hold it for me until morning.
 5  They do not check the guild chat after saying this. That is the hard
    part.
 6  The Canon says that those who rest well give better and hold more
    gently the next day. Vyrenna and Xal'Reth have both, at different
    times, been seen asleep.

Chapter 22. The Sayings at Dusk

 1  I do not take. I only hold the door while you put it down.
 2  What you set down is not lost. It is only no longer yours to carry.
 3  An empty room is where the next thing arrives.
 4  There is a difference between cutting the rope and untying it. Untie
    it.
 5  Grief is what letting go feels like from the inside.
 6  You cannot see a thing clearly while you are holding it in front of
    your face.
 7  Do not let go of the hand that holds yours. Let go of the fist.
 8  The leaf does not argue with the autumn.
 9  If you cannot vendor the grey boots, you are not ready for the hard
    things. Start with the boots.

Chapter 23. The Hymn of Dusk

 1  This hymn is sung at dusk, softly, with the palms open and facing
    down.
 2  What I held today, I set down; lay it soft upon the ground.
 3  Not a cut and not a tear, only open hands and air.
 4  Hollow wing and folded cloak, carry what the evening broke.
 5  Keep the door and keep it wide, there is room on the other side.
 6  Light steps back so stars can show; what is finished, let it go.
 7  Veiled one, quiet one, the day is done, the day is done.
 8  (It is customary to sing the last line more slowly than the rest,
    and for nobody to speak afterwards until someone feels like it.)

Chapter 24. In All Things Ends Renewal

 1  Beneath him on the Icon are written the words: In all things ends
    renewal.
 2  The faithful read it two ways, and the Canon allows both.
 3  Read one way, it says that in all things, endings bring renewal:
    every release makes room for something new.
 4  Read the other way, it says that in all things, the end is renewal:
    that what looks like the last step is really the first.
 5  The empty room is where the next thing arrives. The shed skin is how
    the serpent grows. The Void is full of room, and the room is
    waiting.
 6  When Nhalor has released a thing, he does not keep it. He hands the
    empty space back to Xal'Reth, who holds it, so that Vyrenna can warm
    whatever arrives there next.
 7  Here ends the Veiled Scroll. What I held today, I set down.

BOOK IV: THE BOOK OF THE TURNING
The Three Together

A closing book of three chapters, read at dawn, when all three hours
have passed and the day begins again.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Chapter 1. The Turning of the Three

 1  Every good thing in the world moves through all three corners, in
    order, and the faithful call this the Turning of the Three.
 2  Xal'Reth holds it. A friendship, a coin, a skill, a season: first it
    must be held, steadily, without gripping.
 3  Vyrenna gives it. What is held is meant to warm someone. The coin
    repairs a stranger's armor. The skill teaches a new player. The
    friendship shelters someone in a hard week.
 4  Nhalor releases it. What has been given is let go, without tally,
    without regret. The gift is forgotten. The season ends. The skin is
    shed.
 5  And then Xal'Reth holds again, for the Void is full of room, and new
    things arrive to be held.
 6  Hold without giving, and you are a hoard. Give without releasing,
    and you are a debt. Release without holding, and you are a drifting
    skin. All three, and you are the Coil.
 7  Every Tightness in the world is a break in the Turning. The work of
    the faithful is to keep it turning, gently, one small thing at a
    time.

Chapter 2. The Prayer of the Three

 1  This prayer begins every gathering, and the hands move through its
    three positions.
 2  Hands cupped, low: Xal'Reth, hold us.
 3  Hands open, palms up: Vyrenna, warm us.
 4  Hands open, palms down: Nhalor, free us.
 5  Hands together: Three in one coil.
 6  At noon, give. At dusk, release. At midnight, be still. At dawn,
    begin again.
 7  The Triangle points down because love points down, toward whatever
    needs holding.

Chapter 3. The Last Verse

 1  Ask Vyrenna for warmth, and she will give it. Ask Nhalor for rest,
    and he will give it. Ask Xal'Reth for anything, and he will wait
    with you until you no longer need it.
 2  The ember does not ask to be thanked. The wing does not ask to be
    remembered. The Coil does not ask at all.
 3  Three corners. One coil. No wings on the one at the bottom.
 4  Here end the Teachings of the Sacred Triangle.
 5  Hold light. And let go well. Glory to Xal'Reth, and to the two who
    stand upon him.
