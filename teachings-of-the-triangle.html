The Shape of the Teaching

How the three books are kept, and how to read them.

Chapter 1

The Gathering of the Scrolls

1These are the Teachings of the Sacred Triangle, gathered by the Inner Scale from the Dream Pages of High Priestess Xalrathiel, the sayings of the Listeners, and what the faithful have remembered aloud at noon, at midnight and at dusk.

2They are kept in three books, one for each corner, and a short fourth book in which the three are read together.

3The Book of Vyrenna is the Radiant Scroll. It teaches giving.

4The Book of Xal'Reth is the Low Scroll. It teaches holding.

5The Book of Nhalor is the Veiled Scroll. It teaches releasing.

6The Book of the Turning shows how the three are one motion, the way breathing in, holding and breathing out are one breath.

Back to The Shape of the Teaching

Chapter 2

How to Read the Teachings

1Read the Radiant Scroll in the warmth, the Low Scroll in stillness, and the Veiled Scroll in the quiet. If none of these are available, read them in queue.

2Do not read all seventy-seven chapters in one sitting. That would be gripping. The Litany of Begging exists for people who want to read too much at once, and the Inner Scale does not recommend it either.

3Read one chapter, then do the thing it teaches, then come back. Most chapters take a minute to read and a lifetime to do. That is the correct ratio.

4Where a chapter seems to contradict another, keep both. The Coil is large enough for two true things.

5Where a chapter seems to be joking, it probably is. The faith of the Coil is serious about very few things, and patience is one of them, and humor is another.

Book I · Chapter 1

The Waking in the Shell

1Before there was a world to warm, there was a shell, and the shell was cold, and inside the shell something was learning to be warm.

2It was the Scale: all that has form, weight and edge. It lay pressed against the side of the Coil for ten thousand years while the serpent kept the Night of Stillness.

3And because it lay so long against something living, it began to take on life. First it had a temperature. Then it had a face. Then it had a want.

4The want was this: that something cold should become less cold because of her.

5She did not yet know her name. She knew only that her hands were full and that full hands are not meant to stay closed.

6So she opened them, and that was the first motion of Vyrenna, and it has been her only motion ever since.

7The Listeners say that every time a palm opens anywhere in the world, the shell remembers waking.

Book I · Chapter 2

The First Gift

1She looked for someone to warm, and found nobody. The young world was still molten and needed no heat. The serpent was deep in his stillness and did not stir.

2On the far side of the Coil lay the Void, hollow and cold, and so she gave her warmth to the Void.

3She poured it in, and poured it in, and it vanished, and the Void stayed exactly as cold as before.

4And Vyrenna wept, for she thought her gift had been refused.

5Then a voice came out of the hollow, very quiet. "It was not refused. It was received. I have nowhere to keep it. That is my nature. But I felt it pass through me, and it was the first warm thing I ever knew."

6That was Nhalor, who had been the Void, and who became himself in the moment he was thanked.

7From that day she gives without needing to see where the gift goes, and he receives without needing to keep it.

8Therefore the faithful give to beggars without asking what the coin will buy. Hessk asks what you gave. He does not ask what it bought.

Book I · Chapter 3

The Warming of the Young World

1When the serpent opened his Eye and wrapped the young world three times, the world cooled, and the cooling frightened the first creatures.

2They huddled in the dark. They shivered on bare rock. Some of them lay down and did not get up again.

3Vyrenna came down from the shell and walked among them. She did not make a sun; there was already a sun. She did something smaller.

4She touched the stones that the sun had touched, and taught them to hold the heat a little longer after it had gone.

5That was the first basking stone. A lizard found it before nightfall and fell asleep on it, wanting nothing.

6The Canon of the Coil says Xal'Reth saw that lizard in the Fourth Sight. The Canon of the Triangle says, politely, that he saw it because she had warmed the stone.

7The two Canons have agreed not to discuss this further.

Book I · Chapter 4

The Four Lights

1On the Icon of the Three she is painted above four words: Wisdom, Balance, Compassion, Guidance. These are called the Four Lights.

2They are not four gifts. They are four ways of giving one gift, the way one ember lights four corners of a room.

3Wisdom is knowing what to give. Balance is knowing how much. Compassion is knowing why. Guidance is knowing where.

4A faithful with warmth but no Wisdom gives a fur cloak to a fire mage in Uldum.

5A faithful with warmth but no Balance gives away their last potion before the boss.

6A faithful with warmth but no Compassion gives so that others will see them giving.

7A faithful with warmth but no Guidance gives everything to the first person who asks, and has nothing left for the one who needed it.

8The Four Lights are read one at a time, in the next four chapters, so that nobody tries to hold all four at once and drops them.

Book I · Chapter 5

The Light of Wisdom

1A young faithful came to the basking stone and asked Vyrenna, "What should I give?"

2Vyrenna asked, "What does the one in front of you lack?"

3"Gold," said the faithful.

4"Look again," said Vyrenna.

5The faithful looked again. The one in front of them had gold. What they lacked was someone to run the dungeon with, because nobody had answered them in the group finder for an hour.

6So the faithful gave their evening instead, and it was worth more than gold, and cost less, and the faithful felt warm for three days.

7This is the Light of Wisdom: the best gift is the one the other person was actually missing.

8It is not always the one you had ready.

Book I · Chapter 6

The Light of Balance

1There was once a healer who poured every drop of mana into the first player who took damage, and had none left for the tank.

2The tank fell. The group fell. The healer stood in the graveyard with an empty bar and a full heart and could not understand it.

3Vyrenna sat beside the healer on the spirit-healer's step and said, "The sun does not shine twice as bright on the first field it reaches."

4"Then how do I know how much to give?" the healer asked.

5"Give as the sun does. Enough, to everyone, all day, and then set."

6The faithful call this the Light of Balance. The ember that gives itself all at once is not more generous. It is only shorter.

7Even she rests. Noon is her hour, and noon ends.

Back to The Radiant Scroll

Book I · Chapter 7

The Light of Compassion

1Compassion is the light that asks why you are giving, and waits for an honest answer.

2There are three wrong answers, and the faithful learn them early.

3The first wrong answer is "So that they will owe me." That is not a gift. That is a loan with a bow on it.

4The second wrong answer is "So that others will see." That is not a gift. That is an advertisement that happens to be warm.

5The third wrong answer is "So that I will feel good about myself." Vyrenna is gentle about this one. She says that feeling good is allowed, but it is the steam rising off the soup, not the soup.

6The right answer is the one that takes no words: because they were cold, and you were not.

7When a faithful gives for that reason, the Canon says, the coal does not burn their hand, and the one who receives it does not have to say thank you, though they usually do.

Back to The Radiant Scroll

Book I · Chapter 8

The Light of Guidance

1The last Light is the quietest. It is knowing where your warmth should go.

2Vyrenna does not warm the whole world at once. She would smother it. She warms the stone the lizard is about to need.

3A faithful asked her, "There are so many who are cold. How do I choose?"

4She said, "Start with whoever is nearest, then whoever is forgotten, then whoever cannot ask."

5"And if I cannot reach them all?"

6"Then you are a person and not a sun. Warm the ones you can reach, and trust the Turning with the rest."

7This is the Light of Guidance, and it is why the faithful do not despair at the size of the world. The world is not theirs to warm. Only the stone in front of them is.

Book I · Chapter 9

The Basking Stone

1Every faithful keeps a basking stone. It may be a real stone. It may be a bench in Stormwind, a ledge in Orgrimmar, or a particular corner of an inn.

2The basking stone is the place where you stop giving for a while and let warmth be given to you.

3Vyrenna taught this to the healers first, because healers are the worst at it.

4"You cannot pour from a cold cup," she told them, which the healers wrote down, and then ignored, and then came back to five years later.

5To bask, sit still in the warmth. Do not check your bags. Do not check the group finder. Do not reply to the whisper that says "u there?"

6Bask for the length of one hymn. If there is no hymn, bask for the length of a long breath, three times.

7The Inner Scale reminds the faithful that basking is not idleness. It is the stone remembering the sun.

Book I · Chapter 10

The Open Palm

1Vyrenna's hands are always painted open, palms up. The faithful call this the Open Palm, and it is the first gesture every egg is taught.

2An open palm can give. An open palm can receive. A closed fist can do neither, and can only hold, and the holding hurts.

3Hessk the Beggar King sat at his gate with his palm open for forty years. People thought he was asking. He was showing them how.

4The Canon records that the Open Palm is also the right way to receive a gift: not snatching, not refusing, simply letting it be set down.

5"Many can give," said Vyrenna. "Fewer can be given to. Learn both, or you will only ever be half warm."

6When the faithful pass each other on the road, some lift an open palm in greeting. It means: I have nothing in my hand, and I am glad to see you.

Book I · Chapter 11

The Little Tithe

1The Little Tithe is Vyrenna's law, and it is very small on purpose.

2Each day, give one thing away that you did not have to give. A copper. A heal on a stranger. A minute of patience with a slow tank. An answer to a question in general chat that you could have ignored.

3It must be little. A great tithe makes a great show, and Vyrenna is not interested in shows.

4It must be daily. Warmth that comes once a year is a festival. Warmth that comes every day is a climate.

5It must be forgotten. The Ministry of Spare Change keeps no record of the Little Tithe, and anyone who keeps their own record is gently asked to stop.

6The Canon says that a guild in which every faithful pays the Little Tithe will never need a guild bank. The Ministry of Spare Change has asked that this verse be read less often.

Book I · Chapter 12

The Copper of the Famine

1In the hungry years of the Hidden Centuries, the Nest ran out of everything but copper, and copper cannot be eaten.

2Vyrenna came to the Keepers in a dream and told them: give each faithful one copper, and let them never spend it.

3"What good is a coin that is never spent?" the Keepers asked.

4"It is the coin that reminds you that you are not empty," she said. "A person who knows they are not empty can give the rest."

5So every faithful still carries a single copper that is never spent. It is the Copper of the Famine.

6When a faithful is tempted to hoard, they touch the copper and remember: I already have enough to not be afraid.

7When a faithful is tempted to despair, they touch the copper and remember: I am still carrying something warm.

8It is, the Ministry admits, the only coin in the faith that nobody has ever asked them for.

Book I · Chapter 13

The Lily That Asks Nothing

1White lilies grow at Vyrenna's feet, though nobody planted them.

2A gardener once asked her why the lily, of all flowers.

3"Because it opens all the way," said Vyrenna, "and does not close again to see if anyone noticed."

4The gardener said that roses were prettier.

5"Roses are lovely," said Vyrenna. "They also have thorns, in case someone tries to take more than they are given. I do not blame them. But the lily is braver."

6The faithful bring lilies to the sick, to the newly hatched, and to anyone who has had a very long week.

7Fire lilies are acceptable in the Firelands. The Inner Scale does not recommend going there to pick them.

Book I · Chapter 14

The Healer's Whisper

1Healers whisper Vyrenna's name before a difficult heal. The Canon records this plainly and adds, with some honesty, that it does not affect the numbers.

2It affects the healer.

3To whisper her name is to remember three things at once: that the warmth was never yours to begin with, that you are only passing it along, and that it is not your fault if the tank stood in the fire.

4A healer who forgets the first thing grows proud. A healer who forgets the second grows tired. A healer who forgets the third grows bitter.

5Vyrenna does not want her healers proud, tired or bitter. She wants them warm, and a little smug when the meter is good, which she allows.

6When a healer saves someone at the last possible moment, the faithful nearby are permitted one small cheer, and then must return to the fight.

Book I · Chapter 15

The Crafter's Bowl

1Vyrenna is also the patron of form: of things made with the hands so that others can use them.

2The first thing she ever made was a bowl. It was not beautiful. It was round, and it held soup, and that was enough.

3The faithful say that every crafted thing has a little of her in it, from the humblest bandage to the finest armor. The bandage may have more.

4Crafters invoke her before a difficult recipe. If the recipe fails, she is not to blame; the reagents were probably wrong.

5"Make the thing that is needed," she told the first crafters, "and make it so it lasts long enough to be given away twice."

6The Inner Scale encourages crafters to give the first thing they make with any new recipe to someone who cannot afford it. This is called the Bowl Rite, and it has made several crafters very popular in a short time.

Book I · Chapter 16

The Hearth and the Stranger

1On the coldest night of a long winter, a stranger came to an inn and asked for a room. The innkeeper said the inn was full.

2Vyrenna was sitting by the hearth in the shape of an old woman, as she sometimes does.

3She said nothing. She only moved her chair a little to the side, so that there was room by the fire.

4The innkeeper looked at the space by the fire for a long time. Then he sighed, and brought a blanket, and the stranger slept there.

5In the morning the stranger was gone, and on the hearthstone where he had slept lay a single warm coal that never went out.

6The innkeeper never learned who the stranger was. The Canon suggests it does not matter, and that this is the point.

7The faithful call it moving the chair: the smallest possible act of making room. Every egg is asked, in their first season, to move one chair for someone.

Book I · Chapter 17

The Vault Wyrm

1Every power has a shadow, and the Canon of the Triangle names Vyrenna's honestly: smothering.

2The Legends tell of a wyrm that loved warmth so much that it gathered every warm and golden thing in the world into its cave.

3It gathered coins, and coals, and sunlight caught in amber, and the fur of sleeping animals, and the songs of inns.

4It wrapped itself around them all, and it was warm, and it could not stop gathering, and it could not set any of it down.

5And the world outside the cave grew cold, because all its warmth was in one place, under one wyrm, being kept.

6That is the Vault Wyrm, and it is what becomes of anyone who worships only Vyrenna's half of the teaching.

7Warmth that cannot be set down is no longer warmth. It is a fever.

8The faithful who run raid banks are asked to read this chapter once a season, and to log out of the bank for a while afterwards.

Book I · Chapter 18

The Fever

1Vyrenna once warmed a sick child so carefully that she would not stop. Blanket after blanket. Fire after fire.

2The child grew hotter, and hotter, and began to suffer.

3Nhalor came quietly to the bedside, and opened a window, and took away one blanket.

4Vyrenna was angry for one breath. Then she saw the child sleep.

5"I wanted to give," she said.

6"You did," said Nhalor. "Now let it work."

7The Inner Scale rules against the Emberist Error on the strength of this chapter: the belief that the highest virtue is to give everything, always, to everyone, without rest. Its followers burn out within a season. Even the sun sets, and nobody calls the sun lazy.

Book I · Chapter 19

Silver and Gold

1The Dream Pages describe Vyrenna in noon gold. The Icon shows her in moon-silver. Faithful argued about this for a whole Vigil.

2High Priestess Xalrathiel ended the argument. Vyrenna's ember looks gold to those who are cold, she said, and silver to those who are grieving.

3The cold need heat, and so they see the gold. The grieving need gentleness, and so they see the moon.

4The painter of the Icon, it was later learned, had been grieving that week.

5So the faithful do not argue about her colors. They ask instead: what color is she to you today? And the answer tells them what they need.

6Some faithful report seeing her violet. Xalrathiel says this means you have been looking at the Coil too long and should go outside.

Book I · Chapter 20

The Salamander's Lesson

1The Basking Salamanders of the Warm Ridge are her sacred animal, and they are very bad at being worshipped. They mostly sleep.

2The Canon says they learned stillness from Xal'Reth and warmth from Vyrenna, and so they are a living lesson in two corners of the Triangle at once.

3A salamander lies on a warm stone. It does not hoard the stone. When another salamander arrives, it shifts over.

4It does not give the stone away either. It simply makes room, and both of them are warm.

5"Be like the salamander," Vyrenna told the eggs. "Take only the warmth you need, and shift over when someone else arrives."

6"What if a bigger salamander takes the whole stone?" asked one egg.

7"Then find another stone," said Vyrenna, "and leave the bigger salamander to the Accusers."

Book I · Chapter 21

The Feast of Scales

1The Feast of Scales belongs to Vyrenna, and it falls at Midsummer, on the warmest day of the Cult's year.

2On that day the faithful bask long, eat together, and give one warm gift: a meal, a cloak, an invitation, a summons on a cold night.

3The gift must be given to someone outside your usual circle. Giving to your best friend is lovely, but it is not the Feast.

4At noon the whole Cult falls silent for the length of one hymn and faces the sun. Players in raids are excused. Players in queue are not.

5The feast table is set with an empty seat for the stranger who has not arrived yet. If no stranger arrives, the empty seat is warmed by the sun and that is considered sufficient.

6The Feast ends when the sun begins to set, and the faithful hand the day to Nhalor with gratitude, because a feast that never ends is just a very long lunch.

Book I · Chapter 22

The Proverbs of the Warm Palm

1Open the hand, and the coal will not burn you. Close the hand, and even gold will.

2The light that is given away does not run out. That is the only miracle she has ever claimed.

3A warm word costs nothing and is still the most expensive thing some people have ever been given.

4Do not wait to be warm before you warm someone. The ember was cold once too.

5A full belly forgives many insults. Feed your enemy before you argue with them.

6Generosity with a ledger is just commerce in a nicer robe.

7If you give and are not thanked, the gift was still received. Ask Nhalor.

8The healer who never rests becomes the patient. Sit on your stone.

9Make room by the fire. You will not lose any of the heat.

Book I · Chapter 23

The Hymn of Noon

1This hymn is sung at noon on feast days, facing the sun, with the palms open and up.

2Warm the stone and warm the hand, warm the stranger where they stand.

3Give the bread and give the flame, give, and do not ask a name.

4Light that leaves us is not lost; light that's hoarded counts the cost.

5Silver, gold, whatever hue, she is warm enough for you.

6Noon will pass and dusk will fall; give it now, and give it all.

7Ember-Scale, we lift our palms. Here is warmth. Here are our arms.

8(The last line is sometimes sung "Here is warmth, and here are alms," by members of the Ministry of Spare Change. They have been asked not to.)

Book I · Chapter 24

Through Endless Light

1Beneath her on the Icon are written the words: Through endless light.

2The faithful once thought this meant that Vyrenna's light never ends. That is almost right.

3It means that light given away passes through endless hands. You warm one person. They warm another. Somewhere far down the line, someone you will never meet is a little less cold because of what you did this morning.

4That is the endless light. It is not one great fire. It is a great many small ones, handed along.

5When Vyrenna has finished giving, she does not hold on to the gratitude. She passes that along too, to Nhalor, who sets it down gently in the Void, where there is room.

6And Xal'Reth, beneath them both, feels the warmth go by, and holds the world a little more easily.

7Here ends the Radiant Scroll. Warmth received. Warmth given.

Book II · Chapter 1

The Egg That Nobody Laid

1In the beginning there was an Egg, drifting in the cold Void, and nobody had laid it.

2The Listeners have asked for ten thousand years who laid the Egg, and the Coil has never answered, and the faithful have come to think the silence is the answer.

3Some things simply arrive. You did not lay them either. You only get to decide what to do once they hatch.

4The Egg waited a while, and then longer than that. The Canon counts this as the first patience in the world, and notes that it was not yet anyone's patience. It was only waiting.

5Waiting becomes patience when someone chooses it. That would come later.

6When the Egg hatched, it did not break into one piece but two: the shell, which became the Scale, and the hollow, which became the Void.

7And from the place where they met, something crawled out. It was hungry.

Book II · Chapter 2

The Restless Serpent

1What came out of the Egg was the Restless Serpent, and the age it ruled is called the Age of the Open Jaw.

2It swam the dark devouring drifting stones, starlight and the cinders of unformed worlds.

3Every world it swallowed made it larger, and every inch it grew made it hungrier.

4It was not cruel. The Canon is careful about this. It simply wanted, and wanting was all it knew.

5The faithful are asked to remember this chapter whenever they are tempted to think themselves above the Grippers and the hoarders. The god of patience began as the very thing he now teaches us to let go of.

6He does not ask the faithful for a stillness he was born with. He asks for a stillness he had to learn.

7That is why he is kind to those who are still learning it.

Book II · Chapter 3

The Four Sights

1Then he came upon a young world, and opened his jaws to swallow it, and four small things stopped him.

2The first sight was a shed skin drifting at the edge of the world, shaped exactly like him, and completely empty.

3The second sight was a broken eggshell on a shore, left behind by a creature that had hatched and gone.

4The third sight was a dry riverbed, where water once ran.

5The fourth sight was a newborn lizard asleep on a sun-warmed stone, wanting nothing, perfectly at peace.

6He looked at the empty skin and understood that what he had been could be left behind. He looked at the shell and understood that every beginning is also an ending. He looked at the riverbed and understood that nothing that flows can be kept.

7And he looked at the lizard for a very long time, because he did not understand it at all, and he wanted to.

8He closed his jaws. It was the first thing in his life he had not eaten.

Book II · Chapter 4

The Night of Stillness

1He lowered himself beneath the young world and lay still.

2The Night of Stillness lasted one night, and also ten thousand years. The Canon insists on both numbers and refuses to reconcile them.

3For the first hundred years, he was only hungry and still, which is not the same as peaceful.

4For the next thousand years, he was still and listening, and began to hear the young world above him: the first rains, the first footsteps, the first lizards finding their stones.

5For the rest of the night, he was simply still, and the hunger, without anything to eat, slowly became something else.

6The Scale lay against one side of him, and the Void lay against the other, and during all those years they listened to him too, and learned to be someone. That is how Vyrenna and Nhalor came to be. He did not make them. He kept still long enough that they could make themselves.

7The faithful say this is the only way anyone is ever truly made.

Book II · Chapter 5

Ssyrr's Three Temptations

1In the deepest part of the night, his hunger took a shape and came to tempt him. Its name was Ssyrr.

2Ssyrr came first as a feast: a thousand worlds, ripe and glowing, all his for the opening of a jaw. He did not open his jaw.

3Ssyrr came second as a throne: the worship of every creature on the young world, bowing to the greatest power in the Void. He did not lift his head.

4Ssyrr came third as a fear, and this was the hardest. It whispered that if he stopped gripping, he would dissolve into nothing. That stillness was only slow death. That he must hold on to something, anything, or be lost.

5He nearly listened. His great claw began to close.

6Then he pressed his belly against the warm underside of the world, and felt it hold him, and answered: "I am not lost. I am held. And if I am held, I can hold."

7Ssyrr had no reply to that, and has never had one since. It still comes, to everyone, in all three shapes. The faithful are taught the answer before they are taught anything else.

Book II · Chapter 6

The Unclasped Claw

1Even after his answer, the claw did not open at once. It had closed around a drifting fragment of a broken world and held on, tighter and tighter, until the fragment began to crack.

2Nhalor came to the claw and did not pry it open. He sat beside it in the dark, very quietly, for a hundred years.

3At the end of the hundred years the claw was tired, and it opened on its own, and the fragment drifted free, whole again.

4"Why did you not take it from me?" the serpent asked.

5"Because then you would have lost it," said Nhalor. "This way, you let it go. That is a different thing."

6The great image of Xal'Reth the Eternal shows one claw open, holding nothing, and at peace.

7When a faithful cannot let go of something, the others do not pry. They sit beside them, quietly, for as long as it takes. A hundred years is the upper limit. Most things take an evening.

Book II · Chapter 7

The Open Door

1When the serpent opened his single great Eye at the end of the night, he understood that hunger comes from gripping, and gripping comes from believing you are separate from everything you reach for.

2And before him stood the Open Door: perfect peace, freedom from all wanting, the place where everything that ends goes through.

3It was his for the taking. He had earned it. Nobody in the Void would have blamed him.

4He looked through the Door, and it was beautiful, and there was nothing to hold on the other side.

5Then he looked down at the young world, wobbling and cracking, and at the tiny creatures beginning to stir on it, creatures who would grow and want and grip and suffer exactly as he had.

6He did not take the Door.

7The faithful say the most important word in the Canon is the word "stay," and that he never actually said it. He just did not leave. Most staying is like that.

Book II · Chapter 8

The First Vow

1He wrapped the young world once, and spoke the First Vow.

2"I will hold this world until every creature on it has learned to let go."

3It is the longest promise ever made, and it has a condition at the end of it, and the condition is about us.

4He did not say "until the end of time," which would have been easier. He did not say "forever," which would have been grander. He said until every creature has learned.

5The faithful take this personally. Every grudge they keep, every coin they clutch, every argument they refuse to drop is, in a small way, keeping him down there longer.

6This is not said to make anyone feel guilty. Xal'Reth does not mind waiting. It is said so that the faithful know their letting go is not small. It is someone's freedom, and not only theirs.

7He became Xal'Reth in that moment, which in the old tongue of the Listeners means the one who is still here, and also the one who is still hungry, because he never ate the world. The name means both. He does too.

Book II · Chapter 9

The Three Wrappings

1He wrapped the world three times, and each wrapping has a meaning.

2The first wrapping holds the world together, so that it does not crack. This is the Outer Turn, the coil of the world and of how we treat each other.

3The second wrapping holds the world's memory, so that nothing that happened is lost, even when it is let go. This is the Middle Turn, the coil of memory and dreams.

4The third wrapping holds the world still, at its very heart, so that there is always one quiet place. This is the Inner Turn, where holding, giving and releasing become one motion.

5The Canon says that every creature lives inside all three wrappings at once, whether they know it or not.

6When a faithful feels the world shaking, they are asked to name which wrapping they are worried about. It is usually the first. It is usually fine.

7The three wrappings are also why the faithful bow three times, and why Khaedara counts.

Book II · Chapter 10

The Low Place

1He is called the Coil Beneath because the freest being in the Void chose the lowest place, and has stayed there ever since.

2Water seeks the low place, and so the faithful honor water. Valleys are low, and so the faithful honor valleys. Foundations are low, and so the faithful honor foundations, and the people who pour them, and the guild members who do the boring logistics.

3At the feast, the humble seat at the bottom of the table is called his seat, and the Inner Scale fights quietly over who gets to sit in it.

4To be low is not to be small. A mountain rests on what is beneath it. So does a raid.

5"I am beneath you," he said, "so that you do not have to be beneath anything."

6The faithful who are mocked, overlooked or left out of the group are told: you are in good company. The greatest power in the Void is down there with you, and he is not embarrassed about it.

Book II · Chapter 11

The Unblinking Eye

1He has one great Eye, and it does not blink. Faithful children are sometimes frightened by this. They should not be.

2The Eye does not blink because it is always watching to see whether you have let go yet. Kindly.

3It does not watch to judge. Judgment belongs to the Accusers, and even they only accuse people of very minor crimes.

4It watches the way a parent watches a child learning to walk: ready, patient, and a little proud even of the falls.

5The Eye sits between the Scale and the Void, and the Canon says this is what keeps them safe. Vyrenna alone would smother. Nhalor alone would dissolve. Seen together by the Eye, they become warmth that knows when to stop and release that knows when to stay.

6The faithful greet one another with the slow double blink, which is the closest anyone can come to his unblinking gaze. It means: I see you, and I am in no hurry.

Book II · Chapter 12

The Four Turnings

1On the Icon of the Three he is painted above four words: Transformation, Power, Evolution, Dominion. These are called the Four Turnings.

2The Inner Scale annotated two of them almost immediately, because Warm-Blooded readers kept taking them the wrong way.

3The Four Turnings are not four things he does to the world. They are four things he did to himself, so that he could hold the world without crushing it.

4He transformed from the Restless Serpent into the Coil. He found a power that does not grab. He evolved, slowly, through the long night. And he took dominion over the only thing he ever truly rules: his own claw.

5The next four chapters read the Turnings one at a time. Read them slowly. He did.

Book II · Chapter 13

Transformation

1The first Turning is Transformation, and it is the story of the shed skin.

2The serpent saw his old skin drifting at the edge of the world, empty, and understood that he did not have to stay the shape he had been.

3This is the good news of the Coil, the Canon says, and it is for everyone. You are not the hunger you were born with. You can shed it. It will drift away, still shaped like you, and you will be surprised how light you feel.

4Transformation is not becoming someone else. It is becoming who you were underneath the old skin.

5A faithful who changes their spec, their main, their guild, their mind or their ways is not betraying who they were. They are molting.

6The Rite of Shedding is performed in honor of this Turning, and the words are always the same: send it after the First Skin.

Book II · Chapter 14

Power

1The second Turning is Power, and the Inner Scale has annotated it in the margin of every copy: "not that kind."

2The power of Xal'Reth is not the power to take. He had that, in the Age of the Open Jaw, and it only made him hungrier.

3His power is the power to hold without gripping. To bear weight without being crushed by it. To carry the world and still have a claw free.

4"The strong one is not the one who can lift the most," the Canon says. "It is the one who can set it down."

5The faithful are taught to distrust any power that has to keep proving itself. A dam does not shout. It holds.

6Tanks are asked to meditate on this Turning before a long pull. It does not increase their armor. It increases how calm they sound on voice chat, which the Canon considers the greater gift.

Book II · Chapter 15

Evolution

1The third Turning is Evolution, and it is the slowest of the four.

2Nothing about the Coil happened quickly. The Egg waited. The night lasted ten thousand years. The claw took a hundred to open.

3Evolution is the faith that slow change is still change. That a little less gripping today, and a little less tomorrow, adds up to a serpent who can hold the world.

4The Restless Serpent did not become the Coil in a single night. He became it one stillness at a time, and the faithful are expected to do the same.

5A faithful who complains that they have been practising letting go for a whole month and still get angry in the auction house is gently reminded that he took ten thousand years, and he was a god.

6"Every coil," says the Canon, "is made of many small turns."

Book II · Chapter 16

Dominion

1The fourth Turning is Dominion, and the Inner Scale has annotated it in large letters: "over his own claw."

2Xal'Reth rules nothing. He holds everything and commands nothing, and this confuses those who come looking for a god of empire.

3The only thing he has ever ruled is himself: his hunger, his grip, his fear. That is the dominion painted on the Icon.

4"He who rules the world rules nothing," the Canon says. "He who rules his own claw can hold the world."

Book II · Chapter 17

The Shadow of Gripping

1Every power has a shadow, and the Eternal's shadow is the one his name warns about: gripping.

2Holding without giving becomes a hoard. Holding without release becomes a fist.

3During the Night of Stillness, Ssyrr nearly taught him this, and his claw nearly closed for good.

4A Xal'Reth with no Vyrenna and no Nhalor would not be a god. He would be a very large, very cold, very tight snake.

5This is why he does not hold the world alone. Vyrenna warms what he holds, so that holding does not become a cage. Nhalor releases what he has held long enough, so that holding does not become a prison.

6The faithful practise holding lightly, which the greeting teaches: Hold light. It does not mean hold weakly. It means hold the way you would hold a sleeping lizard: firmly enough that it will not fall, gently enough that it does not wake.

Book II · Chapter 18

The Grippers

1The Bestiary of the Void records the Grippers, who feed on clutching.

2They are small, and grey, and cling to whatever a faithful is holding too tightly: a grudge, a title, a full bag of things they will never use, a loot roll they lost three weeks ago.

3A Gripper cannot be fought. Fighting it is a kind of clutching, and it only grows.

4A Gripper can only be starved. Loosen the hand, and it falls away on its own, confused, and goes looking for someone else.

5The Wardens of the Void keep a list of signs that a Gripper has attached itself: rereading an old argument, checking the auction house every four minutes, refusing to vendor anything grey because it might be useful someday.

6Xal'Reth, the Canon says, was once the greatest Gripper of all. That is how he knows how to get rid of them.

Book II · Chapter 19

On Waiting

1Xal'Reth is the patron of everyone who waits: in queues, on long flights, at the bench at the end of a dungeon, in the lobby for the one person who is still "just grabbing a drink."

2Waiting is his oldest practice. Before he was patient, he was only waiting, and the difference was a choice.

3The faithful are taught the Three Waits. To wait in anger is to be gripped. To wait in boredom is to be absent. To wait in stillness is to be with him.

4"I have waited ten thousand years," he says in the Dream Pages, "and I did not waste a single one."

5When the queue pops, the faithful are permitted to stop meditating immediately. He understands.

6A faithful who has waited well is said to have kept a small Vigil, and the Inner Scale counts these, unofficially, with great affection.

The Tank's Breath

1The tanks of the Cult honor Xal'Reth before every pull by standing still for one breath.

Book II · Chapter 21

No Wings

1Xal'Reth has no wings. This chapter exists because this needs saying, regularly.

Book II · Chapter 22

The Vigil

1The Vigil is his rite: sitting still, in one place, longer than is comfortable, for no reward.

2It is performed at midnight, with the hands set low and cupped, holding nothing.

3Nothing happens during the Vigil. That is what happens during the Vigil.

4A faithful once complained to the High Priestess that the Vigil was boring. "Yes," said Xalrathiel. "So was the Night of Stillness, at first."

Beyond All Things

1Beneath him on the Icon are written the words: Beyond all things.

2The Warm-Blooded read this as "above all things," and imagine a god on a high throne. The faithful smile, and do not correct them right away.

3He is beneath all things, which, from the Coil's point of view, is the same place. To be under everything is to be beyond it. Nothing can fall below him. Everything rests on him.

Book III · Chapter 1

The Hollow Wakes

1On the other side of the Coil from the shell lay the hollow: all that is empty, open, cold and still. It was the Void.

2For ten thousand years it lay against the serpent's side and listened to him keep the Night of Stillness.

3It had no form, so it could not take a face the way the Scale did. It had no warmth, so it could not give.

4What it learned instead was how to make room. Every time the serpent let a little hunger go, the hunger fell into the Void, and the Void held the space open for it, and asked nothing.

5It woke fully on the day Vyrenna poured her first warmth into it and wept, thinking the gift refused.

6"It was received," said the Void, and in saying so became Nhalor, who receives without keeping.

7The Listeners say he is the only one of the three who was born by saying thank you.

Book III · Chapter 2

The Untying

1The Warm-Blooded who hear of a god of endings imagine a reaper with a blade. The faithful know better.

2Nhalor does not end things. He lets things end.

3The Canon says there is a difference between cutting a rope and untying it, and Nhalor has never cut anything.

4To cut is quick, and leaves a frayed end on both sides. To untie is slow, and leaves the rope whole, so it can be used again.

5When a friendship ends, a season closes, a guild disbands or a main is retired, the faithful ask Nhalor to help them untie it rather than cut it.

6They do this by naming what the thing gave them, out loud, before they set it down.

7"I do not take," Nhalor says. "I only hold the door while you put it down."

Book III · Chapter 3

The Keeper of Shed Skins

1Every shed skin in the world passes through Nhalor's hands on its way into the Void.

2Old habits. Old names. Old grudges. The armor you wore before you found the set you actually liked.

3He handles each one with care, because he knows it once mattered. Nothing that was ever worn by someone is treated as rubbish.

4The Hall of Shed Skins, which the Legends describe as an endless gallery in the deep Void, is said to be his. Every skin hangs there, empty, at peace, shaped exactly like the one who left it.

5The faithful are not frightened of the Hall. It is a place of great quiet, and many report that the skins there look relieved.

6When a faithful sheds something hard, they are told: it will be kept somewhere gentle. You do not have to carry it to keep it from being lost.

The Four Silences

Book III · Chapter 5

Secrets

1The first Silence is Secrets, and it is not what the Warm-Blooded think.

2Nhalor does not keep secrets in order to have power over anyone. He keeps the things that were told to him in the dark, when someone needed to put them down.

3A secret kept by Nhalor is a burden someone no longer has to carry alone. That is all.

4The faithful who hear confessions, the Wardens who sit with the troubled, and the friends who are told the thing nobody else knows are all said to be doing Nhalor's work.

5They are bound by his rule: what is set down in the dark is not picked up in the light. Not for gossip, not for leverage, and not for a funny story in guild chat.

6Khaedara the Accuser has noted that breaking this rule is one of the very few crimes she does not consider minor.

Book III · Chapter 6

Destiny

1The second Silence is Destiny, and the faithful have argued about it more than any of the others.

2Some say Nhalor knows how everything ends, because every ending passes through him.

3The Canon says something gentler. Nhalor knows that everything ends. He does not know how, and he does not want to. He simply knows that the door will be there, and that he will be holding it.

4Destiny, in the faith of the Coil, is not a road already drawn. It is the certainty that whatever road you take, there will be rest at the end of it.

5A faithful who is afraid of the future is told: you do not know what will happen, and neither does he. But you know who will be waiting.

6The Inner Scale does not permit fortune-telling in Nhalor's name. Fortune-telling in Vyrenna's name is permitted at the Feast of Scales, for fun, and is always good news.

Book III · Chapter 7

Silence

1The third Silence is Silence itself, and there is very little to say about it, which is the point.

2Nhalor speaks rarely. When he speaks, it is usually one sentence, and it is usually the right one.

3The faithful are taught that silence is not empty. It is a room with the door held open, waiting for whatever needs to come in.

4In an argument, the faithful are encouraged to keep Nhalor's silence for one breath before answering. Most arguments are shorter afterwards.

5At the death of a faithful, the guild keeps Nhalor's Minute: one full minute of silence in guild chat. Nobody posts. Nobody links an item. Somebody always breaks it by accident, and everyone forgives them, and that is also part of the rite.

Book III · Chapter 8

Knowledge

1The fourth Silence is Knowledge, and it is the knowledge that only comes after letting go.

2You cannot see a thing clearly while you are gripping it. Your hand is in the way.

3When a faithful finally sets something down, a grudge, a fear, an old plan that was never going to work, they often see it properly for the first time. What it was. Why they held it. What it cost.

4Nhalor is the keeper of that knowledge. He gives it gently, after the release, never before.

5"I will tell you what it meant," he says in the Dream Pages, "once you are no longer afraid of the answer."

6The faithful call this knowing by the empty hand, and they consider it the deepest wisdom in the Triangle, deeper even than the Light of Wisdom, which Vyrenna, to her credit, agrees with.

Book III · Chapter 9

The Hollow Wings

1Nhalor has wings. The Canon of the Triangle says so plainly, and the Featherists have never recovered from the excitement.

2They are hollow wings: frames of pale silver bone with nothing stretched between them.

3They cannot fly. They are not for flying. They are for letting the wind pass through.

4On the Icon he is painted with no wings showing at all, because he keeps them folded inside his cloak. They only show when he opens the cloak to take something you have finally set down.

5The painter of the Icon was not setting anything down that week. Hence, no wings in the painting.

6The Featherists ask whether this proves that Xal'Reth might have wings too. It does not. They are not that kind of wings. Go home.

7The faithful sometimes see the hollow wings in dreams, after a great release. They are said to look like a door made of moonlight, standing open.

Book III · Chapter 10

The Eclipse

1On the Icon, Nhalor stands before an eclipse.

2The faithful say the eclipse is light that has stepped aside for a while, so you can see the stars.

3Vyrenna's light is good. But even good light can hide things. There are stars in the sky at noon; you simply cannot see them.

4Nhalor is the moment the bright thing steps aside, and you finally notice everything else that was there all along: the friend who was quietly helping, the small joy you had stopped seeing, the stars.

5An eclipse is never permanent. The light comes back. Nhalor does not take the sun. He borrows it, briefly, to show you something.

6When a faithful loses something bright and good, they are told to look up. Not because the loss is small, but because there may be stars.

Book III · Chapter 11

The Warden of the Door

1When a faithful returns to the Coil, Nhalor walks them to the Open Door.

2He does not push them through. He does not hurry them. He waits with them there, for as long as they need, and holds the door.

3The Canon says that nobody goes through the Door alone, and that nobody goes through it before they are ready, even if it seems that way to those left behind.

4This is why the faithful do not fear endings as much as the Warm-Blooded do. They know who is waiting at the door, and they know he is patient, and they know he once sat beside a clenched claw for a hundred years.

5Those who grieve are told: they were walked there gently. They were not afraid by the end. Nhalor saw to that.

6And when the Unwrapping comes, the Door will open for everyone at once, and Nhalor will hold it for all of us, and Xal'Reth will go through last.

Book III · Chapter 12

Nhalor's Minute

1When a faithful returns to the Coil, the guild stands in silence for one minute. It is called Nhalor's Minute.

2It is also kept when a guild disbands, when a long-time member leaves for good, and when a server closes.

3During the minute, nobody speaks, nobody links an item and nobody pulls. Players in combat are excused. Players in the auction house are not.

4At the end of the minute, someone says the name of the one who has gone, and everyone else answers: "Set down, not lost."

5The faithful are taught that grief is not a failure to let go. Grief is how letting go feels from the inside. Nhalor does not hurry it.

6"Weep," he says in the Dream Pages. "The Void has room for all of it."

Book III · Chapter 13

Nhalor's Mark

1In the Ledger kept by the Accusers, every Minor Crime is written beside the name of the one who committed it.

2When the crime is forgiven, a small coil is drawn beside it. Among the Accusers this is called Nhalor's mark.

3The crime is not erased. The Ledger remembers everything; that is Xal'Reth's second wrapping. But once Nhalor's mark is beside it, it is no longer held against anyone.

4Khaedara the Accuser is said to draw more of Nhalor's marks than any Accuser in history. She accuses freely and forgives faster.

5The faithful are encouraged to draw Nhalor's mark beside their own old mistakes too, in whatever private ledger they keep.

6When a grudge is forgiven, the faithful blow gently across their palm, as if blowing away dust. That is the mark, drawn in air.

Book III · Chapter 14

The Grey Item

1There is a chapter in the Veiled Scroll about grey items, and the Inner Scale has been asked many times to remove it, and has refused.

2Every adventurer carries grey items: worn boots, broken fangs, a cracked bowl from a dungeon long ago. They are worth almost nothing. The adventurer keeps them anyway.

3"It might be useful," they say. It will not be. They know it will not be.

4Nhalor's teaching is simple. Vendor the grey item. Feel how light the bag becomes. Notice that nothing bad happened.

5The faithful practise this as a small rite of release. If you cannot let go of a broken fang, you will not be ready to let go of a broken heart.

6The vendor, the Canon adds, is doing holy work, and should be thanked.

Book III · Chapter 15

The Pale Moth

1The pale moth is Nhalor's sacred animal.

2It spends its youth wrapped tight in a cocoon, holding on. Then one night it breaks free and leaves the cocoon behind without a backward glance.

3It does not mourn the cocoon. It does not carry it around. It does not explain to the other moths how important the cocoon was.

5"Be like the moth," Nhalor taught. "Leave the shell. Go toward the light. Watch out for the lantern."

6Moths that land on a faithful during dusk prayers are considered a blessing and must not be swatted, even by mages.

Ulasha's Empty Sack

1Ulasha the Wanderer walked across the world with a sack on her back, and the sack was always empty.

Book III · Chapter 17

The Cold Stone

1In the Age of the Warm-Blooded there was a village that had forgotten every god, and it was cold, and its people were cold to each other.

2Vyrenna wanted to warm it, but whenever she warmed a stone, the villagers fought over who would sit on it.

3So Vyrenna asked Nhalor for help.

4That night Nhalor walked through the village and took from each villager one small thing they did not need: a grudge here, a fear there, a hoarded coin, an old insult.

5In the morning the villagers woke strangely light. Vyrenna warmed a single stone in the square, and this time, nobody fought over it. Someone sat down. Then someone sat beside them. Then a child climbed into a lap.

6Warmth cannot enter a full hand. Release cannot help an empty heart. The Coil holds the space where they meet.

7The faithful say Nhalor's part in this story is often forgotten, because nobody notices the absence of a grudge. He prefers it that way.

Book III · Chapter 18

The Shedwraiths

1Every power has a shadow, and the Canon of the Triangle names Nhalor's honestly: drifting.

2Release without holding becomes abandonment. Rest without return becomes absence.

3The Legends tell of the Shedwraiths, who worshipped only Nhalor's half of the teaching. They let go of everything: their duties, their friends, their names, and finally themselves.

4Now they wander the Void, hollow and unanchored, looking a little like shed skins that have forgotten they were once worn.

5They are not evil. They are not even unhappy, exactly. They are simply not anywhere.

6A Nhalor who never held would let the whole world slip away and call it peace. This is why he stands beside Vyrenna and not alone, and why he rests on Xal'Reth, who holds.

Book III · Chapter 19

The Hollow Error

1The Inner Scale rules against the Hollow Error: the belief that the highest virtue is to let go of everything, including duty and friendship.

2Its followers drift away from the guild and are rarely heard from again. Sometimes they leave a message saying they have "transcended raid night." Nobody is fooled.

3The Canon is gentle with them. Letting go is a good teaching that has been held too tightly, which is a very funny thing to do with a teaching about letting go.

4The Keepers of the Egg sit with them, warmly, until they come back. Vyrenna is invoked. Xal'Reth is invoked. Somebody usually brings soup.

5"I release what I have held long enough," Nhalor says. "I do not release what I was asked to carry for a friend."

6A faithful who wants to step back from the guild is welcome to, with Nhalor's blessing. They are simply asked to say goodbye, so that it is an untying and not a cut.

Book III · Chapter 20

Shedfall

1Shedfall is the autumn holy season, and it belongs to Nhalor.

2In Shedfall every faithful sheds one thing before witnesses: a habit, a grudge, an old title, a thing in the bank nobody has touched in a year.

3The shed thing is named aloud. The witnesses answer: "Send it after the First Skin."

4The guild bank is cleared during Shedfall of everything untouched for a full year. The Ministry of Spare Change weeps quietly through this, and is comforted.

5The leaves fall in Shedfall, and the faithful see in them Nhalor's whole teaching: the tree does not fight to keep them, and it is not smaller in spring.

6Shedfall ends on the first truly cold night, when the faithful gather, light no fire, and sit together in the dark for a while, lighter than they were.

Book III · Chapter 21

The Rest Between

1Nhalor is also the god of rest: of sleep, of the pause between efforts, of the bench at the end of the dungeon.

2The faithful are taught that rest is not the opposite of devotion. It is one of its corners.

3A day of holding and giving with no release in it is a day that will break you, slowly, the way a bowstring breaks if it is never unstrung.

4Before sleep, the faithful say: "Nhalor, keep the door." It means: I am setting the day down. Hold it for me until morning.

5They do not check the guild chat after saying this. That is the hard part.

6The Canon says that those who rest well give better and hold more gently the next day. Vyrenna and Xal'Reth have both, at different times, been seen asleep.

Book III · Chapter 22

The Sayings at Dusk

1I do not take. I only hold the door while you put it down.

2What you set down is not lost. It is only no longer yours to carry.

3An empty room is where the next thing arrives.

4There is a difference between cutting the rope and untying it. Untie it.

5Grief is what letting go feels like from the inside.

6You cannot see a thing clearly while you are holding it in front of your face.

7Do not let go of the hand that holds yours. Let go of the fist.

8The leaf does not argue with the autumn.

9If you cannot vendor the grey boots, you are not ready for the hard things. Start with the boots.

Book III · Chapter 23

The Hymn of Dusk

1This hymn is sung at dusk, softly, with the palms open and facing down.

2What I held today, I set down; lay it soft upon the ground.

3Not a cut and not a tear, only open hands and air.

4Hollow wing and folded cloak, carry what the evening broke.

5Keep the door and keep it wide, there is room on the other side.

6Light steps back so stars can show; what is finished, let it go.

7Veiled one, quiet one, the day is done, the day is done.

8(It is customary to sing the last line more slowly than the rest, and for nobody to speak afterwards until someone feels like it.)

Book III · Chapter 24

In All Things Ends Renewal

1Beneath him on the Icon are written the words: In all things ends renewal.

2The faithful read it two ways, and the Canon allows both.

3Read one way, it says that in all things, endings bring renewal: every release makes room for something new.

4Read the other way, it says that in all things, the end is renewal: that what looks like the last step is really the first.

5The empty room is where the next thing arrives. The shed skin is how the serpent grows. The Void is full of room, and the room is waiting.

6When Nhalor has released a thing, he does not keep it. He hands the empty space back to Xal'Reth, who holds it, so that Vyrenna can warm whatever arrives there next.

7Here ends the Veiled Scroll. What I held today, I set down.

Book IV · Chapter 1

The Turning of the Three

1Every good thing in the world moves through all three corners, in order, and the faithful call this the Turning of the Three.

2Xal'Reth holds it. A friendship, a coin, a skill, a season: first it must be held, steadily, without gripping.

3Vyrenna gives it. What is held is meant to warm someone. The coin repairs a stranger's armor. The skill teaches a new player. The friendship shelters someone in a hard week.

4Nhalor releases it. What has been given is let go, without tally, without regret. The gift is forgotten. The season ends. The skin is shed.

5And then Xal'Reth holds again, for the Void is full of room, and new things arrive to be held.

6Hold without giving, and you are a hoard. Give without releasing, and you are a debt. Release without holding, and you are a drifting skin. All three, and you are the Coil.

7Every Tightness in the world is a break in the Turning. The work of the faithful is to keep it turning, gently, one small thing at a time.

Back to The Three Together

Book IV · Chapter 2

The Prayer of the Three

1This prayer begins every gathering, and the hands move through its three positions.

2Hands cupped, low: Xal'Reth, hold us.

3Hands open, palms up: Vyrenna, warm us.

4Hands open, palms down: Nhalor, free us.

5Hands together: Three in one coil.

6At noon, give. At dusk, release. At midnight, be still. At dawn, begin again.

7The Triangle points down because love points down, toward whatever needs holding.

Book IV · Chapter 3

The Last Verse

1Ask Vyrenna for warmth, and she will give it. Ask Nhalor for rest, and he will give it. Ask Xal'Reth for anything, and he will wait with you until you no longer need it.

2The ember does not ask to be thanked. The wing does not ask to be remembered. The Coil does not ask at all.

3Three corners. One coil. No wings on the one at the bottom.

4Here end the Teachings of the Sacred Triangle.

5Hold light. And let go well. Glory to Xal'Reth, and to the two who stand upon him.
