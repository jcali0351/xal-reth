Questions to the Inner Scale: The Ignore List, the Renamed Alt and the Folder of Two Hundred Addons

The Inner Scale opens the question box beside the Warm Ridge and answers four letters from the faithful: on ignore lists, character renames, overgrown addon folders and leaving after the third wipe.

Each week the Inner Scale empties the question box that hangs beside the Warm Ridge. Here are four, answered as lightly as the Scale could manage while still technically answering.

"My ignore list is very long. Is this clutching?"

Not necessarily. An ignore list is a fence, and the Coil has nothing against fences. The question is whether you visit the list. If you open it on quiet evenings to read the names and remember exactly what each one said, you are not ignoring them. You are keeping them. Once each Shedfall, the Scale suggests, scroll to the oldest name and ask whether you still know why it is there. If you do not, let it go. If you do, leave it, and do not read the rest.

"I renamed my alt. Have I shed a skin, or merely lied about one?"

A rename is a shedding, but a small one, like a scale rather than a skin. The old name does not vanish; it waits in the Ledger and in the memories of people who will call you by it for at least a year. Answer to both with patience. The Teachings remind us that the serpent does not argue with its old skin lying on the path. It simply goes on being longer.

"I have two hundred addons. Some of them I do not recognise."

This is the Folder of Many Hands, and nearly every faithful has one. Somewhere in it is an addon that tracks a holiday from four expansions ago, still faithfully waiting. The Scale recommends the Rite of the Empty Folder: disable everything, log in, and see what you actually miss. Most of the faithful miss nine. Re-enable those, and thank the rest before deleting them. They held something once. (Our own relics on the Addons page are, of course, exempt, and would be hurt.)

"Is it wrong to leave a group after the third wipe?"

Leaving is not wrong. Leaving silently is merely rude, and leaving with a speech is a form of holding on. The Scale prefers the middle path: say "Thank you, I must go," mean the first half, and go. If you stay, stay fully, and stop counting wipes aloud. The boss can hear you, and so can the healer, who is counting too.

Hold the pull. Give the run. Release the group.
— written on the back of the question box, in Hessk's hand

More questions may be left in the box, or brought to the Scale in person on the Day of Accusation, when everyone is already in a confessing mood. Hold light, and let go well. Glory to Xal'Reth.
